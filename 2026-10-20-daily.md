## 1. Risk Watch

### 1.1 BeyondTrust Remote Support CVE-2026-102764, China-nexus zero-day (NEW)
BeyondTrust disclosed the critical flaw (advisory **BT26-05**, public **15 Oct**) after exploitation as a zero-day starting about **29 Aug**, affecting one BeyondTrust-hosted and one self-hosted instance in the first known cases. Affected versions are reported as **26.2.3 and earlier**; CVSS 4.0 **9.3**. Mandiant ties the campaign to **ARMORY CASTLE (formerly UNC5221)**, a suspected China-nexus espionage cluster whose playbook (edge and remote-access appliances, long dwell time) resembles earlier Ivanti and similar campaigns. SaaS instances were patched around **8 to 9 Oct**; self-hosted customers must patch manually unless auto-update is on. BeyondTrust is withholding technical details until self-hosted appliances are patched. Shadowserver counts over **1,400** exposed instances, with no patch-rate data. Patch dates differ between outlets.
**Action:** Patch self-hosted Remote Support now per BT26-05, treat any internet-reachable appliance that ran a vulnerable build since late August as potentially compromised (review sessions, new accounts, outbound connections, rotate stored credentials and API keys), and remove public exposure where possible.
Sources: [SOCRadar](https://socradar.io/blog/cve-2026-102764-beyondtrust-zero-day/) · [CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4236421/beyondtrust-zero-day-tied-to-chinese-spies-puts-remote-support-users-on-alert.html) · [Cybersecurity Dive](https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/china-espionage-beyondtrust-zero-day-hacker/833240/) · [BleepingComputer](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/beyondtrust-patches-zero-day-exploited-in-attacks-since-august) · [BeyondTrust BT26-05](https://beyondtrust.com/blog/entry/bt26-05)

### 1.2 Cisco Secure Email Gateway CVE-2026-76519 (EXPLOITED, KEV)
A relative path traversal in the Advanced Malware Protection (AMP) feature allows unauthenticated remote command execution with root privileges. An appliance is exposed when **AMP File Reputation** is assigned to a mail policy and **Content Scanner Tools** is older than **25.3.0.5525**. Cisco's PSIRT says it learned of exploitation in **October**; it names no actor and gives no victim count. CISA added it to KEV with a federal deadline of **17 Oct**. The primary fix is updating Content Scanner Tools to **25.3.0.5525+** (no restart); fixed AsyncOS builds are reported as **15.5.5-014** and **16.5.0-780**, but one outlet puts those fixes in September, and I could not confirm a fix for the 16.0 branch. Shadowserver sees about **300** exposed instances. Cisco's cloud service is already fixed.
**Action:** Update Content Scanner Tools now. Cisco says patching does not remove an intrusion and that upgrading a compromised box destroys evidence: check for compromise first, rebuild compromised appliances, rotate credentials, and restore every member of a cluster that contains a compromised node (cluster SSH keys).
Sources: [CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4235801/cisco-warns-of-actively-exploited-secure-email-gateway-flaw.html) · [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/cisco-warns-of-exploited-secure-email-gateway-vulnerability/) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/cisco-warns-of-active-attacks.html) · [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/?p=200985)

### 1.3 Ivanti Connect Secure chain, CVE-2026-104935 (ONGOING, follow-up)
No change found. Volexity's **UTA0576** chain (auth bypass in welcome.cgi plus file write in wu.cgi, tracked as **CVE-2026-104935**, CVSS **8.6**) still ends in a root escalation via the dsinvoked/DSCache flaw that reportedly has **no CVE and no fix** ("coming weeks"). Fixes for the first two are in **22.8R2.5, 25.1.1.2, 25.1.3.0, 25.1.3.1**. The Integrity Checker Tool does not detect the chain. Exploitation dates from **1 Oct** (Defused) or **3 Oct** (Volexity). Newest coverage I found is about 13 Oct.
**Action:** Upgrade, remove internet exposure of portal and management interfaces, treat pre-patch exposure as potential compromise (memory and network forensics, rotate credentials and certificates held on the device), and check Ivanti's advisory daily for the escalation fix.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/attacker-chains-three-ivanti-connect.html) · [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/security/ivanti-zero-day-july-cisa-patch-no-fix/) · [iTnews](https://itnews.com.au/news/attackers-chain-three-ivanti-vpn-zero-days-for-root-access-629621)

### 1.4 AhsayCBS backup servers, CVE-2026-105133 and CVE-2026-105134 (ACTIVE EXPLOITATION)
Attackers chain an authentication bypass (**CVE-2026-105133**) with command injection (**CVE-2026-105134**; CVSS **10.0** per VulDB via Bitdefender, **9.3** CVSS v4 per Huntress) to take over AhsayCBS, then drop **webshells and XMRig** cryptominers. Huntress saw first exploitation on **7 Oct** (23:20 UTC); Bitdefender has investigated multiple customer incidents since the same date and warns that access can sit idle for weeks before ransomware or data theft. I found no report of ransomware actually deployed. Ahsay's earlier fix was insufficient: servers below **v10.3.4.45** (v10) or **v9.15.2.70** (v7 to v9) are exposed. KEV status is reported inconsistently (CISA added both on 14 Oct per one outlet, other sources said not yet at 12 Oct).
**Action:** Apply the latest hotfix and reboot, hunt for new webshells in the Ahsay web directories and for unexpected XMRig or miner processes, and review backup sets and stored credentials for tampering. Backup consoles hold the keys to your recovery plan, so treat compromise as high impact.
Sources: [Huntress](https://www.huntress.com/blog/ahsaycbs-flaws-exploit) · [Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/blog/businessinsights/technical-advisory-ahsaycbs-exploitation-chain-cve-2026-105133-cve-2026-105134) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/attackers-exploit-ahsaycbs-flaws-to.html) · [SecurityWeek](https://securityweek.com/unpatched-ahsaycbs-vulnerabilities-exploited-in-the-wild)

### 1.5 French supermarket refrigeration attacks, Danfoss controllers (DEVELOPING, follow-up)
Counts remain inconsistent: the finance minister told parliament about **700** of roughly 10,000 supermarkets were affected, other outlets say 350 or more, and Carrefour reported 80 stores. Danfoss says most affected equipment is legacy and unsupported, and names **AK-SM800** and **AK-SC355** regulator systems. A Claroty report from August had flagged many such controllers reachable from the internet. CERT-FR (12 Oct) told operators to disconnect exposed devices or tightly filter access. Intermarché said affected stores must discard fresh and frozen stock. No claim, ransom demand or arrest has been reported, and the entry method is not publicly established; the Danfoss link rests on anonymous sources and vendor statements.
**Action:** Find internet-reachable refrigeration and building-management controllers, put them behind VPN or allow-lists, isolate end-of-life units, replace default or shared credentials, and review logs for unexpected defrost or setpoint changes.
Sources: [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/europe/french-supermarkets-refrigerator-shutdown-cyberattack) · [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/danfoss-fridge-attack-france-supermarket/) · [Beinsure](https://beinsure.com/news/cyberattack-on-french-supermarkets-causes-millions-in-losses/)

## 3. Incident Watch
- **Bitget crypto exchange (follow-up):** About **$387.5M** stolen across **11 blockchains** after attackers compromised two third-party security appliances via zero-days (privileged access on **24 Sep**; earliest linked activity **31 Aug**), then reached the production wallet job server and issued fraudulent withdrawals. Bitget says private keys and cold wallets were not compromised; SlowMist and Mandiant investigate. The vendors of the appliances are not named in coverage I found. Attribution to North Korea rests on on-chain and wallet overlaps and is an assessment, not confirmed. Frozen amounts are reported inconsistently (about $0.6M to $1.1M); the CEO is "not very optimistic" about recovery. [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/bitget-confirms-third-party-zero-day.html) · [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-09-28-bitget-attacker-tested-risk-controls-small-transfers-388-million-theft-ceo-says-417045) · [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bitget-hack-zero-day-slowmist-investigation)

IDCF says data is recoverable only from customers' own backups, with a 14 Oct revision reportedly letting some of the **194** snapshot users get copies back case by case; one source says snapshots cannot be extracted, so this is unresolved. No actor named; the attackers' claims are unverified.
