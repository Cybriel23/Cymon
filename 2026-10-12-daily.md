Tempo is moderate; the dominant story remains exploitation of perimeter and management-plane products. Four items matter most today. (1) **Fortinet FortiMail CVE-2026-104286** (CVSS **9.8**, unauthenticated file write) is exploited and the fixed-build status is still conflicting across sources (**7.4.9 / 7.6.7 / 8.0.2** named; **7.2 has no fix**, upgrade to 7.4+). Caveat: most sources here were reached through search summaries, so verify CVE details and fixed versions against vendor advisories before acting. ## 1. Risk Watch

### 1.1 Fortinet FortiMail unauthenticated file write, CVE-2026-104286 (ONGOING, follow-up)
Advisory **FG-IR-26-175** (CVSS **9.8**, path traversal plus NULL-byte handling) lets an unauthenticated attacker write arbitrary files via crafted HTTP(S) requests; Fortinet confirmed in-the-wild exploitation and CISA added it to KEV on 1 Oct (due 4 Oct). Affected: **8.0.0–8.0.1, 7.6.0–7.6.6, 7.4.0–7.4.8**. Named fixes: **7.4.9, 7.6.7, 8.0.2**. Fortinet listed these as upcoming as of 3 Oct; a Singapore CSA alert dated 6 Oct says updates are released, but I could not confirm that on Fortinet's own page. The **7.2** branch has no fix (upgrade to 7.4+; 7.2 end of support **10 Nov**). Fortinet's interim mitigation: disable Identity-Based Encryption (IBE) support or restrict management-interface access. No actor or victim count disclosed.
**Action:** Check FG-IR-26-175 for the current build and upgrade; apply the IBE/network mitigation meanwhile. Hunt for new files in web-served directories, modified scripts, and requests containing `%00` or `../`.
Sources: [Field Effect](https://fieldeffect.com/blog/patches-pending-actively-exploited-fortimail-flaw) · [Singapore CSA AL-2026-133](https://www.csa.gov.sg/alerts-and-advisories/alerts/al-2026-133/) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/critical-fortimail-zero-day-flaw.html) · [CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/10/01/cisa-adds-one-known-exploited-vulnerability-catalog)

### 1.2 Citrix NetScaler CVE-2026-88771 / -88772 and CVE-2026-88779 (ONGOING, follow-up)
Bulletin **CTX697096** fixes two unauthenticated RCE flaws (both CVSS **9.5**) exploited as zero-days; -88772 requires DTLS (default on VPN vServers). CISA put both on KEV on 27 Sep and advised checking for compromise before patching or rebooting. **CVE-2026-88779** (CVSS **8.7**, SAML service provider/IdP configurations) is used in targeted attacks, mainly causing DoS; researchers are still checking for RCE potential. Fixed: **14.1-73.41** and **13.1-64.28**. Shadowserver counted roughly **22,000** exposed appliances (earlier figure). One report says researchers have published an exploit, which I did not verify.
**Action:** Capture memory/disk before patching or rebooting. Patch, then hunt for webshells, new admin accounts, odd `nsroot` sessions and outbound connections. Rotate LDAP bind, VPN certificate and other secrets reachable from the appliance.
Sources: [Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2026/security/citrix-urges-urgent-netscaler-patch-after-zero-day-attacks-detected) · [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/?p=199873) · [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/citrix-admins-warned-to-shut-down-netscalers-over-2-exploited-zero-days/) · [CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/09/27/critical-zero-day-vulnerabilities-exploited-citrix-netscaler-adc-gateway)

### 1.3 Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager auth bypass, CVE-2026-76504 (ONGOING, follow-up)
CVSS **9.8** API session authentication bypass, exploited since September, on KEV; all deployments affected, **no workaround**. Fixed: **26.2.1, 26.1.2.1, 20.18.4.1, 20.15.6.1, 20.12.8.2, 20.9.10.1**. No attribution; earlier SD-WAN bugs were tied by Cisco to UAT-8616, which is a pattern only.
**Action:** Upgrade, keep the Manager off the internet, and review for unexpected admin accounts, API tokens and config pushes since September.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/cisa-adds-exploited-cisco-catalyst-sd.html) · [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/cisco-patches-exploited-catalyst-sd-wan-zero-day-vulnerability/amp/)

### 1.4 IDCF Cloud (SoftBank) ransomware, Japan (DEVELOPING)
Ransomware hit IDC Frontier's IDCF Cloud on **7 Oct**. Four availability zones (tesla, henry, pascal, joule) in East Japan Region 1 stopped and cannot restart; **495** companies and local governments are affected, with services such as Wi-Fi authentication, cloud telephony, public-sector websites and reportedly railway/police-related systems disrupted. Customers were told to restore from their own backups and rebuild elsewhere. The company isolated the region and suspended management consoles; no unauthorized access confirmed in other zones/regions. The attacker's claims (breach in seven minutes, **225** databases / **3.6 PB** encrypted) come from customer screenshots and are unverified. One report says JR East and View Card records (up to **6.09M**) may be affected; unconfirmed. Exfiltration and intrusion path are still under investigation.
**Action:** If you consume IDCF or any single-provider cloud, verify backups are offline/out-of-tenant and test restores; segregate hypervisor and management-plane credentials.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/ransomware-attack-disrupts-japans-idcf-cloud-used-by-govt-clients/) · [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/security/japanese-cloud-cyberattack-railways-police-food-suppliers-495-organizations-idcf/) · [Nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/en/news/yjj2026100700867/) · [SDxCentral](https://www.sdxcentral.com/news/softbank-national-cloud-arm-attacked-in-seven-minutes-taking-japan-infrastructure-offline/)

### 1.5 Bitget $387.5M theft via third-party security-product zero-days (DEVELOPING, follow-up)
Bitget says attackers used a zero-day in a third-party security product (two products reportedly involved, called only "Product A/B") to reach an internal system and inject fraudulent withdrawals from hot/warm wallets. SlowMist places the earliest malicious activity at **31 Aug**. Attribution to North Korea/Lazarus is suspected from IP behavior, on-chain analysis and wallet overlaps, not confirmed. Frozen funds are small (about **$0.63M–$1.1M**, sources differ). No vendor or CVE has been named as of the latest reporting I found.
**Action:** Treat security appliances as tier-0; add out-of-band approval for withdrawal paths; watch for vendor disclosures matching this pattern.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/bitget-confirms-third-party-zero-day.html) · [Hackread](https://hackread.com/bitget-hack-suspects-north-korea-lazarus-group/) · [Rescana](https://www.rescana.com/post/bitget-cryptocurrency-exchange-breach-387-5m-stolen-via-zero-day-exploit-in-third-party-security-products)
