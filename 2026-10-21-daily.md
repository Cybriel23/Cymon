# Daily Cyber Threat Intelligence Briefing
Wednesday, 21 October 2026 · Coverage: past 24 hours and follow-ups on tracked topics

## Executive Summary
Search coverage of 20 to 21 Oct was again thin, so much of this briefing rests on developing stories from the past week; secondary-source details are flagged where unverified. Four live concerns. (1) A new supply-chain incident: stolen **ConnectWise Cloud Backup (formerly SkyKick)** application credentials were used to obtain Microsoft Graph tokens and sign in to about **173** customer and partner Microsoft 365 tenants from **15 Oct**; the service is suspended, and MSPs should audit their tenants now. (2) Edge-device exploitation continues: **BeyondTrust Remote Support CVE-2026-102764** (China-nexus ARMORY CASTLE, with Mandiant warning of wider opportunistic exploitation now that the flaw is public), **Ivanti Connect Secure** (root escalation still unpatched), and **SonicWall SMA1000 CVE-2026-102255** (CVSS **10.0**, exploitation attempts within two days of the 6 Oct fix). (3) **AhsayCBS** backup servers remain a target; the first patch failed and only hotfixes **10.3.4.45 / 9.15.2.70** work. (4) Retail disruption: **Ahold Delhaize** is still in a "controlled restart" after the 12 Oct incident, and French supermarket refrigeration attacks have no attribution yet. Watch this week for: the Ivanti escalation fix, ConnectWise's account of how the credential was stolen and restoration of Cloud Backup, a ransomware claim (or not) against Ahold Delhaize, the Bitget technical report, IDCF console restoration, and further KEV additions.

## 1. Risk Watch

### 1.1 ConnectWise Cloud Backup (SkyKick) credential abuse in Microsoft 365 tenants (NEW, DEVELOPING)
Huntress observed suspicious sign-ins to customer Microsoft 365 tenants from a hosting-provider IP starting **15 Oct** (about 06:13 to 08:34 UTC), using Microsoft Graph access tokens obtained with credentials tied to the ConnectWise Cloud Backup application identity. First counts of **162** tenants have risen to about **173** affected partner and customer tenants. On **17 Oct** (07:00 UTC) ConnectWise disabled seven application identities and revoked the associated certificates, which suspended Cloud Backup; scheduled backups are not running and restore is unavailable. ConnectWise says archives are encrypted and isolated, with no evidence they were accessed. It has not said how the credential was stolen, has not attributed the activity, and could not determine whether live tenants saw further unauthorized activity. Tokens issued earlier may remain valid until they expire. Counts come from Huntress and secondary outlets and may change.
**Action:** If you use ConnectWise Cloud Backup or SkyKick, review Entra ID sign-in and audit logs from 15 Oct for the ConnectWise/SkyKick enterprise applications, hunt for sign-ins from hosting-provider IPs and for mailbox, SharePoint or OneDrive access via Graph, revoke refresh tokens and sessions, and do not re-enable the apps until ConnectWise confirms all credentials are reset. Plan alternative backup coverage meanwhile.
Sources: [Huntress](https://www.huntress.com/blog/vendor-credential-enables-unauthorized-access) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/connectwise-suspends-cloud-backup-after.html) · [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/stolen-connectwise-skykick-credential-exposed-microsoft-365-tenants-629721) · [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-breach-microsoft-365-tenants-via-connectwise-supply-chain-attack/amp/)

### 1.2 BeyondTrust Remote Support CVE-2026-102764, China-nexus zero-day (ONGOING, follow-up)
Exploited as a zero-day from **29 Aug** (one hosted, one self-hosted instance); BeyondTrust found the hosted activity on **28 Sep**, shipped patch **BT26-05** on **8 Oct** and disclosed the CVE on **15 Oct**. Affected: Remote Support **26.2.3 and earlier**; CVSS 4.0 **9.3**, an authentication bypass leading to unauthenticated file operations, webshells and root. Mandiant/Google attribute it to **ARMORY CASTLE (formerly UNC5221)** and warn the group has historically moved to opportunistic mass exploitation after disclosure. Feedly lists it as CISA KEV; confirm in the catalog. SaaS instances were patched automatically; self-hosted customers must apply BT26-05 manually. Shadowserver counted over **1,400** exposed instances.
**Action:** Patch self-hosted instances now, assume compromise of any exposed appliance that ran a vulnerable build since late August (review sessions, accounts, webshells, outbound connections; rotate stored credentials and API keys), and remove public exposure where possible.
Sources: [SOCRadar](https://socradar.io/blog/cve-2026-102764-beyondtrust-zero-day/) · [BleepingComputer](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/beyondtrust-patches-zero-day-exploited-in-attacks-since-august) · [Canadian Cyber Centre AL26-025](https://cyber.gc.ca/en/alerts-advisories/al26-025-critical-vulnerability-impacting-beyondtrust-remote-support-cve-2026-102764) · [CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4236421/beyondtrust-zero-day-tied-to-chinese-spies-puts-remote-support-users-on-alert.html)

### 1.3 Ivanti Connect Secure chain, CVE-2026-104935 (ONGOING, follow-up)
No fix yet for the third link. Volexity's **UTA0576** chains an auth bypass and file write (**CVE-2026-104935**, fixed in **22.8R2.5, 25.1.1.2, 25.1.3.0, 25.1.3.1**) with a root privilege escalation that has no CVE and no patch ("weeks away"), giving unauthenticated root on an otherwise patched **22.8R2.4** appliance. No webshell is dropped; commands travel inside the exploit's own HTTPS requests, and the Integrity Checker Tool does not detect it. Exploitation began **1 Oct** (Defused) or **3 Oct** (Volexity); Volexity assesses with low confidence that the motive is criminal rather than espionage. CISA lists the CVE in KEV.
**Action:** Upgrade, remove internet exposure of portal and management interfaces, run memory and network forensics on appliances exposed since early October, rotate credentials and certificates held on the device, and watch Ivanti's advisory for the escalation fix.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/attacker-chains-three-ivanti-connect.html) · [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/security/ivanti-zero-day-july-cisa-patch-no-fix/) · [iTnews](https://itnews.com.au/news/attackers-chain-three-ivanti-vpn-zero-days-for-root-access-629621)

### 1.4 SonicWall SMA1000 CVE-2026-102255, CVSS 10.0 (EXPLOITATION ATTEMPTS)
A pre-authentication SSRF in the SMA1000 Work Place interface lets a remote attacker use the appliance as a proxy through an unintended alternate access path. Fixes shipped **6 Oct**: **12.4.3-03670** and **12.5.0-03082** (platform hotfixes) for the 6210, 7210 and 8200v. Previdian and Defused saw attempts from **8 Oct**, with 135 attempts from two US IPs by **12 Oct**; successful compromise is not confirmed, and one outlet says SonicWall had not updated its advisory to mark active exploitation. Note this is separate from CVE-2026-15409 (a June SMA1000 zero-day that CISA flagged as used in ransomware). The unusual CVE format means the ID should be checked against SonicWall's advisory. (Yesterday's briefing cited CVE-2026-15409/15410 for the October exploitation; the October activity appears to concern CVE-2026-102255.)
**Action:** Apply the hotfix, restrict the management and Work Place interfaces, and review logs for unexpected outbound requests originating from the appliance.
Sources: [SecurityWeek](https://securityweek.com/critical-sonicwall-vulnerability-exploited-days-after-disclosure) · [eSecurity Planet](https://www.esecurityplanet.com/threats/news-sonicwall-sma1000-cve-2026-102255-exploitation/) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/critical-sonicwall-sma-1000-flaw.html)

### 1.5 AhsayCBS backup servers, CVE-2026-105133 / CVE-2026-105134 (ONGOING, follow-up)
Auth bypass plus command injection chain drops webshells and **XMRig**; Huntress saw first exploitation on **7 Oct**. Ahsay admitted on **10 Oct** that releases **10.3.4.0** and **9.15.2.59** did not fully fix the flaws; the working fixes are hotfixes **10.3.4.45** (v10) and **9.15.2.70** (v7 to v9), followed by a reboot, and Huntress verified 10.3.4.45 blocks the entry point. Both CVEs were added to KEV on **14 Oct** (federal deadline 17 Oct, passed). Observed payloads are miners and webshells; I found no report of ransomware deployed. Patching does not remove webshells already planted. Affected-version statements differ between Huntress and Bitdefender; use the hotfix levels above.
**Action:** Apply the hotfix and reboot; hunt for unexplained JSP files, unauthorized replication receivers and new admin accounts (Ahsay's indicators); review backup sets and stored credentials for tampering.
Sources: [Huntress](https://www.huntress.com/blog/ahsaycbs-flaws-exploit) · [Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/blog/businessinsights/technical-advisory-ahsaycbs-exploitation-chain-cve-2026-105133-cve-2026-105134) · [eSecurity Planet](https://www.esecurityplanet.com/news/news-ahsaycbs-patch-fails-two-exploited-vulnerabilities/) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/ahsaycbs-flaws-exploited-to-deploy-web.html)

## 5. Miscellaneous
- **Pattern:** In three weeks, BeyondTrust, Cisco SEG, Ivanti, PAN-OS, SonicWall SMA1000 and Ahsay have all been exploited at or soon after disclosure; the ConnectWise case adds a vendor-credential path into M365. Inventory internet-facing management and backup tooling and audit third-party app permissions in your tenants. [Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/4232292/sonicwalls-latest-critical-flaw-indicates-a-security-pattern-not-another-one-off-bug-2.html)
- **Backups as target:** Ahsay, ConnectWise Cloud Backup and IDCF all involve backup or recovery infrastructure; keep an offline or out-of-band copy and test restores. [hard2bit](https://hard2bit.com/en/blog/idcf-cloud-ransomware-snapshots-backups-outside-cloud-provider/)
