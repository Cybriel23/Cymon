### 1.1 FortiMail CVE-2026-104286: there is no patch, and the KEV deadline is tomorrow (CORRECTION, ONGOING, EXPLOITED)

**Correcting yesterday's briefing:** this item was reported with fixed versions of **8.0.2+, 7.6.7+ and 7.4.9+**. Reading Fortinet's advisory directly today, the solution field says **"Upgrade to upcoming [version] or above"** — **no fixed build exists yet**, and SecurityWeek's 2 October coverage states the same: "patches remain unavailable," with Fortinet committing only to include fixes in upcoming versions and **giving no timeline**. Everything else holds: **CVSS 9.8**, **path traversal (CWE-22)** combined with **improper neutralisation of NULL bytes (CWE-158)**, allowing an **unauthenticated** attacker to **write arbitrary files** via crafted HTTP/HTTPS requests, found internally by **Gwendal Guégniaud** of Fortinet Product Security, exploitation confirmed in the wild. **Affected: 8.0.0–8.0.1, 7.6.0–7.6.6, 7.4.0–7.4.8, 7.2.0–7.2.9** — in practice, every supported version. **CISA added it on 1 October with a due date of 4 October**, i.e. a three-day mandate to remediate something with no remediation. Neither Fortinet nor CISA has published exploitation scale, victim count, first-exploitation date or attribution. **Action:** stop waiting for a patch and apply the workarounds as your primary control today — **`config system encryption ibe` / `set status disable`** to remove the exploited feature, and **restrict the management interface to trusted private networks**, which should be permanent regardless. Then hunt, because the published indicators describe full device persistence: **`/data/etc/ld.so.preload`** with an unattributable **`/data/lib/liblog.so`**, replaced **`/data/bin/webconsole`** and **`/data/bin/mailservice`**, modified **`/bin/smit`** and **`/data/etc/httpd.conf`**, a dropped **`/data/migadmin.tar.gz`**, cron entries referencing **`/migadmin`**, and an **`archive234`** account. The exploitation artefact in logs is **IBE decryption errors with invalid Base64 data**. Block and hunt **79.141.169[.]187** and **45.129.0[.]192**. Capture state before you reboot or reimage — an `ld.so.preload` implant disappears with a clean reinstall performed before evidence collection. If you find anything, treat it as exposure of **mail content, routing configuration and every credential the appliance stores or brokers**, and rotate accordingly. For the compliance record, document the workaround as your BOD 26-04 mitigation; that is what CISA's required-action language contemplates when no update exists. **Sources:** [Fortinet FG-IR-26-175](https://www.fortiguard.com/psirt/FG-IR-26-175) · [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/exploited-fortinet-fortimail-zero-day-calls-for-urgent-action/) · [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/02/fortinet-sounds-the-alarm-over-actively-exploited-fortimail-zero-day/5300803) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/critical-fortimail-zero-day-flaw.html)

### 1.2 Zammad CVE-2026-102489 / CVE-2026-102490 added to KEV, deadline Monday — and the vendor says it still has no technical details (NEW KEV, EXPLOITED, follow-up)

Verified programmatically this morning: **CISA added both Zammad CVEs on 2 October with a due date of 5 October**. KEV's own naming is more precise than the original reporting — **CVE-2026-102489** is classified as a **session fixation** vulnerability leading to **remote code execution as the `zammad` user**, and **CVE-2026-102490** as **improper privilege management** allowing the local `zammad` user to **escalate to root**; CISA explicitly notes each can be chained with the other. The operationally decisive new fact is the vendor's position, published on the Zammad community forum and linked from the KEV entry itself: Zammad states it has **not received technical details about CVE-2026-102490 from DIVD**, **cannot verify the claim** that it affects nearly every version ever released, and has **no fix available**, while recommending administrators **update to 7.2.0** (released **23 September**, whose notes do not mention either CVE) and upgrade urgently from **6.5 or older**. Zammad criticises the disclosure process directly — a CVE published without technical detail to the vendor leaves administrators without actionable information. So the practical state is: **a federal three-day patch mandate for a chain whose root-escalation half the vendor says it cannot confirm or fix.** The version data still resolves the chain as half-mitigated: **CVE-2026-102489 affects 6.3.0–6.5.4** and is present-but-not-exploitable in **7.0.0–7.1.3** for environmental reasons, while **CVE-2026-102490 spans v1.5.0 through the v7.1.0 alpha**. Exploitation context is unchanged — the **21 September** intrusion at DIVD, where an agentic attacker ran **password spraying and a man-in-the-middle attack simultaneously**, completing the chain "in seconds," with pivoting to other services and data exfiltration, limited by **network segmentation**. **Action:** treat **7.2.0** as the entry-point fix and nothing more. Get any internet-facing instance behind authentication or offline today; upgrade to **7.2.0**; and until the LPE is fixed, run the Zammad host on the assumption that **any code execution on it equals root** — isolate it, strip stored credentials and API tokens for integrated systems off it, and keep it out of shared trust boundaries. Preserve application and authentication logs **before** remediating, and run **DIVD's `cve-2026-102489_ioc_check_script_v2.sh`**. Because a helpdesk holds ticket attachments, customer correspondence and credentials users paste into tickets, scope any finding as a **data-exposure incident**, not just a server compromise. **Sources:** [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog) · [Zammad community advisory](https://community.zammad.org/t/take-care-local-privilege-escalation-cve-2026-102490-is-reported-as-being-actively-exploited/21297/2) · [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/200248/security/u-s-cisa-adds-zammad-gmbh-zammad-flaws-to-its-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog.html) · [GBHackers](https://gbhackers.com/zammad-vulnerabilities/) · [DIVD-2026-00015](https://csirt.divd.nl/cases/DIVD-2026-00015/)

### 1.3 Dell Container Storage Modules: two CVSS 10.0 authentication bypasses and root on your Kubernetes nodes, upgrade is the only option (NEW)

**Dell** patched **six critical flaws** in **Container Storage Modules (CSM)**, the CSI layer that fronts Dell storage for Kubernetes. **CVE-2026-63688** and **CVE-2026-63692** are both **CVSS 10.0** — **missing authentication** in the authorisation components, letting an **unauthenticated** attacker obtain **full administrative control over the storage infrastructure across all five supported Dell storage product families** and bypass the security model outright. **CVE-2026-67269** (**CVSS 9.9**) is improper privilege management allowing a **low-privilege attacker to escalate to root on Kubernetes cluster nodes** via custom resource manipulation. **CVE-2026-54472** and **CVE-2026-61421** (both **CVSS 9.8**) are **hard-coded credentials and cryptographic keys**, letting an attacker **forge administrative tokens** with no credentials at all; **CVE-2026-67273** (**CVSS 9.6**) is template injection enabling privilege escalation and unauthorised RBAC modification. **All versions before 1.18.0 are affected; 1.18.0 is the only remediation and there are no workarounds.** No exploitation is reported yet. Two reasons to rate this above its newness: hard-coded keys mean **patching alone does not restore trust** — Dell explicitly recommends **rotating JWT signing secrets**, and anything signed with the shipped key must be treated as forgeable until you do; and the blast radius is unusual, because compromising the storage control plane gets an attacker both the data and the ability to attach volumes elsewhere. **Action:** upgrade to **1.18.0** and **rotate JWT signing secrets** in the same change window — the second step is the one teams will skip. Until then, confirm no CSM component is reachable from outside the cluster, restrict access to the CSM namespace and its CRDs to the smallest possible set of service accounts, and review CSM and Kubernetes audit logs for **custom resource modifications** and **authorisation calls without a corresponding authentication event**. Dell's own history is the argument for speed: earlier Dell flaws (**CVE-2021-21551**, **CVE-2026-22769**) ended up actively exploited. **Sources:** [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/dell-csm-flaws-enable-unauthenticated.html)

### 1.4 Kiteworks Email Protection Gateway CVE-2026-54154: unauthenticated root, ~400 exposed instances, and prior zero-day intelligence (NEW)

**CVE-2026-54154** is a maximum-severity flaw in the **Kiteworks Email Protection Gateway** that **combines path traversal, code injection and missing authentication** in a **low-complexity attack requiring no user interaction**, giving an **unauthenticated remote attacker arbitrary code execution and full root control of the appliance**. **Affected: all EPG releases before 9.4.1. Fixed: 9.4.1+.** It arrived inside an unusually large release — **over 100 advisories published 30 September covering 126 vulnerabilities** across **Core, Email Protection Gateway, Secure Data Forms and MFT Server**, of which **11–12 are critical**, concentrated in EPG and enabling **account takeover, code execution and internal network access**. **Shadowserver tracks roughly 400 internet-exposed Kiteworks instances**, patch status unknown. **No exploitation has been reported** — but note the sequence: Kiteworks previously issued a **precautionary instruction to shut servers down** after receiving intelligence about an imminent zero-day, found no evidence of compromise, and has now published the vulnerability that intelligence most plausibly concerned. That is a short fuse between disclosure and exploitation for a product class (secure file transfer and mail gateways) that extortion groups have systematically farmed. **Action:** patch EPG to **9.4.1** this weekend rather than next week, and treat the other critical advisories in the set as part of the same change. Get EPG off direct internet exposure if your architecture allows it. Because the flaw yields root on an appliance that handles your protected mail flows, hunt before and after: review appliance logs for **traversal sequences in request paths**, unexpected process execution and new local accounts, and rotate **any credential, certificate or integration token the gateway holds**. If you were one of the customers who shut servers down on the earlier warning, the honest read is that you now know what you were being warned about and should verify that nothing landed during the exposure window. **Sources:** [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/kiteworks-patches-max-severity-email-protection-gateway-code-injection-vulnerability/) · [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/in-other-news-15k-icloud-spoofing-bugs-ai-policy-experts-phished-adblocker-spies-on-ai-chats/)
