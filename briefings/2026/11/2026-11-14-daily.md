## 1. Risk Watch

### 1.1 Check Point Gaia CVE-2026-123039: KEV-listed with a 16 Nov deadline, chained after PHP2Shell (follow-up, ONGOING)
Check Point's advisory confirms that **CVE-2026-123039** (CVSS **7.8**) was exploited against "a couple of dozen customers". The `dynamic_server` (DynamicCLI) daemon runs as root and listens on `/tmp/dynamic_socket`. The socket is world-writable (**0777**) and does not authenticate callers, so any local user can run commands as root. Affected products are Gaia-based firewalls, Security Gateways, Management/Log Servers, MDS, Scalable Platforms and Maestro Orchestrator on **R81–R82.x**. On its own the flaw requires local code execution. In the campaign, PHP2Shell (**CVE-2026-109618/-109619**) against the Mobile Access portal gave the attackers that foothold. They then planted PHP webshells and the TSGA/TSProxy backdoor, a PhiliKit variant previously seen in UNC5221's Ivanti intrusions. In the Israeli wave they also ran the `destroy_v6` wiper on ESXi hosts and left a `DREAM_ANNEXATION.txt` note. Check Point does not attribute the activity with confidence and lists a false flag or an access broker as possibilities. CISA added the CVE on **13 Nov**, with a due date of **16 Nov**.
**Action:** install the November Jumbo Hotfix on every gateway and management server, starting with internet-facing Mobile Access. If you cannot patch, stop `dynamic_server` and keep it from restarting. Hunt for these signs: commands sent through the socket that bypass the Clish wrapper, use of the `system()` sink, PHP session files containing `upload_progress_pwn`, unexpected `CPconfigdaemon` processes or binaries, gaps in firewall logs, and SSH from a gateway to internal ESXi hosts.
Sources: [BleepingComputer (zero-day)](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/check-point-patches-zero-day-that-lets-hackers-get-root-access/), [BleepingComputer (wiper)](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/check-point-gateways-hacked-via-php2shell-to-deploy-vmware-esxi-wiper/), [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/php2shell-exploited-in-destructive-check-point-gateway-attacks/), [Cyber Security News (KEV)](https://cybersecuritynews.com/check-point-0-day-vulnerability/), [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

### 1.2 ScreenConnect: 2,200+ fake "crypto exchange" guest sessions on 118 self-hosted servers (NEW, developing)
Huntress began investigating on **12 Nov** and found more than **2,200** fake guest sessions on at least **118** self-hosted ScreenConnect servers belonging to **109** accounts. Its own report also gives a figure of 2,408. The sessions have names such as `KUCOIN-SRV` and `LAPTOP-BINANCE`, and the first was created on **3 Nov**. A scripted emulator of the Windows client registers them using relay details taken from publicly downloadable Access installers, which ScreenConnect serves without authentication by design. About **97.8%** of the traffic came from `74.208.158[.]106` (IONOS). The operator sends no payload from the guest side. Huntress's leading hypothesis is that the sessions are bait: a technician who joins with an unpatched client could be hit by **CVE-2026-84869**, the guest-initiated file transfer flaw that SCKit/RelayTrap already uses. Huntress has **not** seen that exploitation yet, and it does not rule out other goals such as capturing keystrokes.
**Action:** upgrade technician clients and Access agents to **26.6.5+**, or remove the `TransferFiles` permission. Back up `App_Data\Session.db` and its WAL file, then delete the fake sessions. Hunt for guests with ClientType 1, an empty public key, or version `25.3.4.9288`. On technician workstations, look for `ScreenConnect.WindowsClient.exe` spawning `cmd`, `powershell`, `wscript` or `msiexec` from `Documents\*\Temp\`. Block `74.208.158[.]106`, `197.47.160[.]252` and `87.106.111[.]220`, and tell technicians not to join sessions they cannot tie to a customer.
Sources: [Huntress](https://www.huntress.com/blog/fake-crypto-exchange-sessions-screenconnect)

### 1.3 UnrealIRCd CVE-2026-114713: any user can run IRCOp commands, KEV due 16 Nov (NEW to KEV)
The bug is an escaping error in UnrealIRCd's IRCv3 `label` message-tag handling. It lets an ordinary user run commands with server or IRCOp authority (KILL, GLINE and more). The maintainer calls it the most critical issue in the project's 15-year history, and upstream reports exploitation on the internet. Versions **6.0.0–6.2.7** are affected. The fix is **6.2.7.1** (released 7 Nov), which can be applied as a hot-patch without a restart. CISA added it to KEV on **13 Nov** with a due date of **16 Nov**. IRC networks remain common C2 and community infrastructure, so a compromised server can also be used to attack its users.
**Action:** hot-patch or upgrade to 6.2.7.1. On UnrealIRCd 5, blacklist the labeled-response module and restart, then plan the move to 6.x. Review oper and ban logs since early November for KILL/GLINE commands issued by non-opers.
Sources: [UnrealIRCd news](https://www.unrealircd.org/index/news), [oss-sec](https://seclists.org/oss-sec/2026/q4/576), [Red Hat Bugzilla](https://bugzilla.redhat.com/show_bug.cgi?id=2568818), [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

### 1.4 Citrix NetScaler ADC/Gateway: 28 flaws, one in the default configuration; NetScaler Console auth bypass (NEW)
Bulletin **CTX697305** (12 Nov) fixes **28** mostly memory-safety bugs. The most urgent is **CVE-2026-120868** (CVSS4 **9.3**, packet-processing overflow), the only one that needs no special configuration. **CVE-2026-107412** (**9.5**) affects Gateway/AAA setups (SSL VPN, ICA Proxy, CVPN, RDP Proxy). Several other **9.3** flaws are in SMPP, DNS/DoH, Push, Email OTP, EPA and ICA proxy. Citrix describes the impact as DoS or "unpredictable behavior" and reports no exploitation. In addition, **CVE-2026-111338** writes authentication data to `/var/log/httperror.log`. Separately, NetScaler Console **CVE-2026-120869** (CVSS **9.4**) is an unauthenticated access-control bypass, fixed in **14.1-74.34**. Fixed ADC/Gateway builds are **14.1-73.57**, **13.1-64.39**, **14.1-73.58 FIPS** and **13.1-37.293** FIPS/NDcPP. There is no workaround. NetScaler memory bugs have often been turned into exploits within weeks of disclosure.
**Action:** patch Gateway/AAA appliances first, then the rest. Update NetScaler Console. After patching, rotate any credentials that may have been logged to `httperror.log`.
Sources: [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/citrix-patches-10-critical-netscaler-vulnerabilities/), [Cyber Security News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/citrix-patches-netscaler-security-vulnerabilities/)

### 1.5 N-able N-central "4EverAble2Shell": still no follow-up hotfix (follow-up, ONGOING)
There was no new N-able fix in the window. The only released build is still **2026.4 HF2** (2026.4.1.26). N-able's 12 Nov notices say the server-side chain depends on the **Legacy API** being enabled and reachable from the internet. N-able also disabled the Intune integration as a precaution and tells on-premises customers to keep servers offline until it gives further guidance. Huntback's report of successful exploitation against HF2 servers (Monero miners) has still not been confirmed by N-able or CISA, and the CVEs are not in KEV.
**Action:** no change from 13 Nov. Keep the Legacy API disabled, keep consoles off the internet, update agents **and** probes, and treat any miner or unexpected root process as a full RMM compromise.
Sources: [Tuskira tracker](https://www.tuskira.ai/advisories/n-able-n-central-able2shell), [SOCRadar](https://socradar.io/blog/4everable2shell-at-a-glance/), [Tenable FAQ](https://www.tenable.com/blog/able2shell-frequently-asked-questions-for-reported-n-able-n-central-zero-day-vulnerabilities)

## 2. Vulnerability Watch
- **CISA KEV, 13 Nov:** two additions, **CVE-2026-123039** (Check Point, see 1.1) and **CVE-2026-114713** (UnrealIRCd, see 1.3), both due **16 Nov**. The deadline is unusually short, which reflects the active exploitation. The Cisco BroadWorks XXE **CVE-2026-20320** (added 10 Nov) remains due **24 Nov**. - **Copeland/Dixell XWEB EVO refrigeration supervisors (14 flaws):** VulnCheck published **14** vulnerabilities that each give root or admin access. Scores run from 8 to **9.8**. One login bug runs the submitted username as a shell command before checking the password. VulnCheck says **at least two** are exploited in the wild. XWEB is one of four systems hit in the October attacks on refrigeration controllers in about 700 French supermarkets. Patches are available, but legacy versions remain exposed. Keep these units off the internet. Exploitation status comes from VulnCheck via CyberScoop and is not yet in KEV. [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/copeland-dixell-refrigeration-vulnerabilities/), [Sekoia (Danfoss campaign context)](https://www.sekoia.io/blog/the-cold-chain-on-fire-unpatched-danfoss-controllers-exploited)
- **AMD SEV-SNP "Mior" and "Skyfall":** two attacks let a malicious host read memory inside confidential VMs. Mior can also modify memory and forge attestation, but needs hypervisor **and** BIOS control: during boot it redirects the PSP's writes to the Reverse Map Table (RMP) into GPU memory. AMD's SEV firmware (Mior) and microcode (Skyfall) are dated 12 Aug. Platform firmware from server makers is due in **November (Skyfall)** and **December (Mior)**. Confidential-computing tenants should track their cloud provider's BIOS rollout. [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/new-mior-and-skyfall-attacks-let-rogue.html)
- **Windows "SignDrop" CLFS LPE (follow-up):** there is still no CVE and no fix. The researcher estimates that **~80%** of installs are vulnerable, namely those where `HKLM\SYSTEM\CurrentControlSet\Services\CLFS\Authentication\Mode` is **0 or 1**. As noted on 13 Nov, do not switch the mitigation off. [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/202968/hacking/new-signdrop-exploit-can-abuse-windows-defender-to-bypass-security-protections.html), [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/nightmare-eclipse-drops-windows-zero-day-exploit-signdrop/)
- **November Windows updates, known issues:** printing fails for documents that contain zero-height or zero-width images (mostly Word over GDI). Activation and KMS commands (`slmgr /skms`, `/ipk`) fail with **0x80041008** on Windows 10 21H2/22H2, Windows 11 23H2 and Server 2019–2025. KMS-activated devices stay activated for up to 180 days, so do not roll back the security update over this. [BleepingComputer (printing)](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/microsoft/microsoft-says-november-security-updates-cause-printing-issues/), [BleepingComputer (activation)](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/microsoft/microsoft-confirms-windows-activation-failures-after-november-updates/)
- **Trezor Safe 5 firmware 2.13.1** (11 Nov) blocks the screen-scraping hardware implants found in CryptoBilis-sold devices (see Incident Watch). Update even if you bought the device elsewhere. [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/cryptocurrency/trezor-confirms-crypto-stealing-implants-in-three-wallets-releases-fix/)
- **Open-source advisories (GitHub, 13–14 Nov):** five High advisories for **nltk** (CVE-2026-126297/-126298/-126299/-126303/-126304), plus **dnspython** CVE-2026-128089, **AutoMapper** CVE-2026-128082 and **ag-psd** CVE-2026-62297. ## 3. Incident Watch
- **Quantum Systems (German military drones), new:** the drone maker confirms a late-October attack by "hostile actors". Der Spiegel and the FT, citing sources, report that a **malicious QBase update** (QBase is its ground-control and mission-planning software) was pushed from the vendor's update server and reached Bundeswehr and other customer servers, though not the drones themselves. The Bundeswehr grounded its fleet as a precaution, and MAD, BfV and BSI are involved. Flights have now resumed. Attribution is open, but the company links the attack to its Ukraine support. The update-server compromise detail is from anonymous sources and has not been confirmed by the company. Defense supply-chain operators should verify the signatures on update channels. [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/german-military-drone-operations-disrupted-by-cyberattack/), [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/13/quantum-systems-drones-back-in-the-air-after-malware-scare/5309658)
- **Trezor / CryptoBilis hardware implants (follow-up):** Trezor has confirmed **three** tampered Safe 5 devices, all bought from Indonesian reseller CryptoBilis between **20 Aug and 23 Sep**. An extra board between the main board and the display captures seed words and passphrases and sends them out over a **cellular** link. Trezor has examined devices from **48** CryptoBilis customers who contacted it, and no fund losses have been reported so far. This follows the confirmed Ledger implants sold through the same reseller. Treat any wallet bought from CryptoBilis as compromised and move funds to a new seed generated on a device bought direct from the vendor. [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/cryptocurrency/trezor-confirms-crypto-stealing-implants-in-three-wallets-releases-fix/), [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/trezor-confirms-hidden-hardware.html)
- **Uber driver data via Fireworks AI (follow-up to the Fireworks/Notion breach):** Uber is notifying US drivers that their data, including **driver's licence** details, was taken from its account with Fireworks AI using stolen provider credentials. Uber learned of it on 9 Oct. State filings so far show **7,870** people (MA, SC, RI), and the total has not been disclosed. There is no evidence that riders were affected. Fireworks AI now has at least two confirmed downstream victims (Notion, Uber). Check your own exposure if you use it as an inference vendor. [Cyber Security News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/uber-confirms-data-breach/)
- **Osaka / Arara cascade (Japan):** the City of Osaka says more than **1 million** email addresses leaked through the **Arara** email-distribution service used by its contractor TKC. Arara's **2 Nov** breach exposed **77 million** addresses from about 400 customers, including Sega, and the service was abused to send phishing emails posing as card companies. Japan's government announced an emergency cyber package worth about **$62M** last week. Expect follow-on phishing that impersonates Japanese municipalities and brands. ## 4. Threat Watch
- **FBI FLASH on the "Artex" AI attack framework:** Artex was released on GitHub in August by "Autumn-27", whom the FBI and The Record identify as Chinese engineer Li Puhua. It lets any LLM chain reconnaissance, exploitation and exfiltration, in some intrusions reaching privileged access in **under three hours**. The repository has been removed, but forks, archived copies and a commercial version are still circulating. Censys counts **1,600+** exposed consoles. In attacker-controlled instances, Chinese models dominate, especially **DeepSeek Flash**. Talos separately maps similar frameworks (Hephaestus, which can be driven from Telegram; PentAGI, with **700+** reachable instances; HexStrike AI over MCP). Defenders should plan for faster, parallel intrusions: tighten exposure management and alert on bursts of scanning followed quickly by exploitation. [The Record](https://therecord.media/artex-ai-tool-fbi-flash-alert), [Cisco Talos](https://blog.talosintelligence.com/artex-and-beyond/)
- **DPRK FaceStealer (follow-up):** the FBI now says the deepfake video-call scheme stole more than **$400M** over 18 months, using stolen webcam clips of **900+** victims to impersonate contacts on fake Zoom calls. The FBI has seized the scheme's infrastructure, and the DOJ separately restrained **$1.2B** from the TraderTraitor Binance theft. Web3 staff should verify meeting links out of band and never run "audio fix" scripts. [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/fbi-says-north-korean-hackers-stole.html), [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/13/fbi-north-korean-fake-zoom-calls/)
- **Operation TorNado:** a Europol/JCODE operation across **14** countries (May 2025 – Oct 2026) arrested **354** vendors and buyers from Archetyp, Nemesis, Tor2Door, Bohemia and Kingdom. More than **€210M** in cash and crypto was seized across Europe and the US, along with about 5.75 t of drugs and 209 firearms. [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/police-arrest-354-vendors-buyers-in-global-cybercrime-crackdown/), [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/13/europol-dark-web-arrests-cybercrime/)

It is an insider-threat case to cite when reviewing who can access seized-asset keys.
