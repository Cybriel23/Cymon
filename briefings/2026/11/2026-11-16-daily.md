# Daily Cyber Threat Intelligence Briefing
Monday, 16 November 2026 · Coverage: 06:00 UTC Fri 13 Nov – 06:00 UTC Mon 16 Nov 2026 and follow-ups on tracked topics

## Executive Summary
The weekend was quieter than last week, but four issues need attention this morning. **First**, CISA's deadlines for two exploited flaws fall **today, 16 Nov**: Check Point Gaia **CVE-2026-123039**, used after PHP2Shell in destructive attacks on gateways and ESXi hosts, and UnrealIRCd **CVE-2026-114713**. **Second**, **NoisyTCP** (**CVE-2026-99288**), a Linux kernel TCP race present since 2015, lets an unauthenticated attacker crash default listeners (nginx, Apache, SSH). It became public on 15 Nov, and fixed stable kernels are available. **Third**, attackers are exploiting the Copeland/Dixell XWEB EVO refrigeration supervisor flaws. Exploitation began within two hours of disclosure, and many legacy builds will never be patched. **Fourth**, a public PoC now exists for **TrustFall** (**CVE-2026-88235**), a Windows servicing-stack privilege escalation that the November update fixes. Among incidents, TSMC confirmed that the employee data being auctioned came from a cloud provider; Maileva (La Poste) disclosed a breach affecting **22M** people; and Coles Group disclosed an incident it is still containing. Watch this week for: distro kernel advisories for NoisyTCP, a possible CISA KEV listing for XWEB EVO, more Tor releases against the relay DDoS, Microsoft's post-incident report on the M365 outage, whether TSMC's data is fully leaked, and any proof from n0n about Expedia.

## 1. Risk Watch

### 1.1 KEV deadlines today: Check Point Gaia CVE-2026-123039 and UnrealIRCd CVE-2026-114713 (follow-up, ONGOING)
Federal agencies must fix both flaws by **16 Nov**, and CISA has added nothing to KEV since 13 Nov. Check Point **CVE-2026-123039** (CVSS **7.8**) is a local root escalation through the unauthenticated, world-writable `/tmp/dynamic_socket` of the `dynamic_server` daemon. It affects Gaia firewalls and management servers, Maestro and Scalable Platforms on **R81–R82.x**. Attackers first got in through PHP2Shell (**CVE-2026-109618/-109619**) on the Mobile Access portal, then used this flaw to gain root, installed the TSGA/TSProxy backdoor, and in one wave wiped ESXi hosts. UnrealIRCd **CVE-2026-114713** (CVSS **8.6**) affects **6.0.0 up to but not including 6.2.7.1**. Unescaped IRCv3 `label` tags let ordinary users inject server-to-server commands on networks of linked servers, and upstream reports exploitation attempts since shortly after the 7 Nov fix.
**Action:** confirm that the November Jumbo Hotfix is installed on every Check Point gateway and management server. Hunt for PHP session files containing `upload_progress_pwn`, unexpected `CPconfigdaemon` processes, and SSH sessions from gateways to ESXi hosts. Upgrade UnrealIRCd to **6.2.7.1**, or set `blacklist-module "labeled-response";` on every linked server.
Sources: [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog), [Security Affairs (KEV additions)](https://securityaffairs.com/203040/hacking/u-s-cisa-adds-check-point-and-unrealircd-flaws-to-its-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog.html), [The Hacker News (PHP2Shell chain)](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/hackers-exploit-php2shell-flaws-in.html), [The Hacker News (UnrealIRCd)](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/attackers-exploit-unrealircd-flaw-that.html)

### 1.2 NoisyTCP CVE-2026-99288: pre-auth remote kernel panic in Linux TCP (NEW)
Researcher Hyunwoo Kim (V4bel) disclosed a race in the kernel's lockless lookup of established TCP sockets after the linux-distros embargo ended. The bug was introduced by commit `079096f103fa` in Oct 2015. A request socket's memory can be freed and reused for a SYN-cookie request while another CPU still holds a stale lookup. The kernel then dereferences a NULL listener and panics. The race works against any plain `listen()` socket, including nginx, Apache and sshd in default configurations. It requires **≥2 RX queues** or RPS. Kim reproduced the crash in **5 s–2 min** on QEMU/KVM guests and in about **14 h** on a quiet bare-metal server. A container crash takes down the whole host. A second path can lead to a use-after-free, but Kim says it is hard to trigger. No public exploit or in-the-wild use has been reported. Red Hat rates the flaw **7.5**. Fixes are in **6.12.117, 6.18.63, 7.3.4** and **7.4-rc2** (upstream fix commit `4e2b3ef19e0e`, "inet: reject unhashed sockets during established lookups").
**Action:** install your distribution's kernel update and reboot, starting with internet-facing VMs and container hosts. Until then, you can either set `net.ipv4.tcp_syncookies=0` in every network namespace, which weakens SYN-flood protection, or reduce NICs to one RX queue with RPS/RFS off. Watch for unexplained reboots and kernel panics in `tcp_v4_rcv` and established-lookup paths.
Sources: [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/kernel-crashing-noisytcp-bug-sat-in-linux-for-11-years-630369), [SecurityOnline](https://securityonline.info/noisytcp-vulnerability-cve-2026-99288/), [Cyber Security News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/noisytcp-linux-kernel-vulnerability/), [oss-security](https://www.openwall.com/lists/oss-security/)

### 1.3 Copeland/Dixell XWEB EVO: two flaws exploited in the wild, legacy fleet unpatchable (follow-up, ONGOING)
VulnCheck's primary write-up gives more detail than last week's coverage. **CVE-2026-124014** (CVSS **9.1**) is an unauthenticated write to `config.lua`. Its NVD record went live at **20:18 UTC on 12 Nov**, and VulnCheck canaries recorded exploitation by **22:00 UTC** (87 hits in the first hour from one source) before any PoC existed. **CVE-2026-124010** (CVSS **9.8**) is a single unauthenticated GET to the setuid-root `xwconsole.cgi` that runs the `sh` parameter as root. Probing for it started at **03:41 UTC on 13 Nov**. VulnCheck reports 14 CVEs in total, including a hardcoded Administrator backdoor password (**CVE-2026-124004**) and pre-auth injection in the login username (**CVE-2026-124002**, for which VulnCheck has a working root exploit). Copeland ended XWEB EVO support in **March 2026**. The `.lua` bug class was removed in the 2021 rewrite, but most exposed units still run pre-2021 builds. Neither exploited CVE is in CISA KEV yet. French authorities attributed October's attacks on supermarket refrigeration controllers to Iran.
**Action:** take XWEB EVO and other refrigeration supervisors off the internet, or put them behind a VPN. Upgrade units that can take **5.6.2/5.7.0+**. In web logs, look for requests to `xwconsole.cgi` with an `sh=` parameter and for writes to `config.lua`. Assume that any exposed legacy unit is compromised and rebuild it.
Sources: [VulnCheck](https://vulncheck.com/blog/copeland-xweb-evo-unauthenticated-root), [SecurityOnline](https://securityonline.info/copeland-xweb-evo-cve-2026-124010-exploited/), [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/copeland-dixell-refrigeration-vulnerabilities/)

### 1.4 TrustFall CVE-2026-88235: public PoC for Windows servicing-stack privilege escalation (NEW)
Two Fudan University researchers have published a write-up and a working tool for **CVE-2026-88235** (CVSS **7.8**). The flaw is a confused-deputy bug in the Component-Based Servicing that TrustedInstaller hosts. CBS exposes an offline-servicing COM interface that does not require admin rights. It accepts a caller-supplied "offline" environment and an unsigned XML manifest, then copies, moves or deletes the files and registry keys that the manifest names as SYSTEM. Any interactive user can therefore escalate. Affected systems include Windows 10 1607/1809/21H2/22H2, Windows 11 23H2 and Windows Server 2016/2019/2022. Microsoft fixed the flaw on **10 Nov** (for example, builds **10.0.19045.7846** and **10.0.17763.9487**). No in-the-wild use has been reported, but local privilege escalations with public PoCs usually show up quickly in post-phishing and ransomware playbooks.
**Action:** make sure the November cumulative update reached all Windows endpoints and servers, especially RDS and VDI hosts with many interactive users. Use EDR to look for non-admin processes that start CBS/TrustedInstaller servicing activity and for unexpected SYSTEM writes under `System32` or to service registry keys.
Sources: [SecurityOnline](https://securityonline.info/trustfall-vulnerability-cve-2026-88235-poc/), [Microsoft Security Update Guide](https://msrc.microsoft.com/update-guide/vulnerability/CVE-2026-88235)

## 2. Vulnerability Watch
- **CISA KEV:** CISA has added nothing since **13 Nov**. The two open items with short deadlines are covered in Risk Watch 1.1. Two VulnCheck-KEV entries for XWEB EVO are not yet in CISA's catalog. [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)
- **Apache Shiro / APISIX / DataSketches (13–14 Nov):** **17** CVEs. The most severe is Shiro **CVE-2026-129349** (CVSS **9.1**): when authentication caching is enabled, `DefaultLdapRealm`/`JndiLdapRealm` accept any password after a user's first successful login. Caching is not the default, and an attacker still needs a valid username. The APISIX gateway fixes include pass-through of client-supplied identity headers (jwe-decrypt/forward-auth, multi-auth), a path-normalisation route-auth bypass, and an `openapi-to-mcp` path escape. Upgrade to **Shiro 3.0.2**, **APISIX 3.20.0** and **DataSketches C++ 5.3.1**. [SecurityOnline](https://securityonline.info/apache-shiro-authentication-bypass-cve-2026-129349/)
- **Tor 0.4.9.17 (emergency relay release):** this release fixes several security issues, notably IPv6 handling in relay DoS defences. It responds to an ongoing flood of client circuits that has affected most relay operators. More releases are expected. Relay operators should upgrade now. Tor Browser is not affected. [Tor Project forum](https://forum.torproject.org/t/security-release-0-4-9-17/22454), [Cyber Security News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/tor-ddos-attack/)
- **Forescout AI-assisted TCP/IP stack research:** Forescout used generally available AI models to confirm **99** vulnerabilities in **10** embedded TCP/IP stacks, **66** of them new, and **52%** of the new ones are high or critical. Some affected stacks belong to discontinued products with no vendor left to patch. Siemens has identified affected **APOGEE, Desigo and TALON** building-automation controllers. Watch Siemens ProductCERT for advisories, and segment building-automation networks in the meantime. [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/16/forescout-ai-models-vulnerability-research/)
- **Open-source advisories (GitHub, 13 Nov):** besides the nltk batch covered on 15 Nov, there are High-severity advisories for **dnspython** (**CVE-2026-128089**), **AutoMapper** for .NET (**CVE-2026-128082**) and **ag-psd** (**CVE-2026-62297**), and Moderate ones for SvelteKit and its adapters and for `@nuxt/nitro-server`. Refresh your lockfiles. [GitHub Advisory Database](https://github.com/advisories)

## 3. Incident Watch
- **TSMC employee data (follow-up):** TSMC confirmed in an SEC filing that data on current and former employees was posted without authorisation. The data matches what is stored with **a cloud service provider**, and TSMC has found no evidence that its own systems were compromised. On **8 Nov** an actor using the name "SmithBianl" posted a 499-row sample and offered for auction a **6.9 GB** set said to hold about **46,000** employee records: national IDs, addresses, contact details and bank data. Bidding started at **3 BTC**, with a free-leak deadline of **15 Nov**. The record count is the actor's claim. TSMC has not named the provider or said whether it engaged with the actor. Expect targeted phishing and fraud against TSMC staff. [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/tsmc-employee-data-breach-linked-to-cloud-provider/)
- **Maileva / La Poste (Docaposte), France:** La Poste confirmed to AFP that an attack in mid-September exposed the names and postal addresses of about **22M** people. Most of them received documents that Maileva sent for its clients, such as social-housing providers, Inovie labs and, via Kiwee.care, patients of Foch Hospital. Maileva says no bank details were exposed, but some payment reminders containing invoice numbers and treatment dates were read. Maileva notified the CNIL, and a criminal investigation is open. No actor has been named. Expect letter- and SMS-phishing that uses real sender context. [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/203126/data-breach/data-breach-at-la-poste-subsidiary-maileva-affects-22-million-people-in-france.html)
- **Coles Group (Australia) (developing):** on 16 Nov the grocery retailer told the ASX it is containing a cyber incident. Stores and online operations are running, and it has so far found no evidence that customer, staff or supplier data was taken. The ACSC and outside responders are involved. No group has claimed the attack. [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/coles-group-investigating-a-cyber-incident-630361)
- **AWS us-east-1 disruption (15 Nov):** from **03:05 to 06:10 PST**, us-east-1 had signup errors, IAM Identity Center console sign-in failures and connectivity problems, and NLB connectivity in one cell was not fixed until 06:21. There is no indication of malicious activity. It is a reminder to check fallback (break-glass) access that does not depend on IAM Identity Center. [Cyber Security News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/aws-services-outage/)
- **Microsoft 365 outage aftermath (follow-up):** at Hotel Ling Bao in Phantasialand (Brühl, Germany), a chlorine-dosing alarm for the pool and sauna sent its alerts only by email. The alerts did not go out during the 12–14 Nov outage, and **15** guests were hurt (4 were taken to hospital). The account comes from local media (WDR), relayed via Notebookcheck. Safety-critical alerting needs a channel that does not depend on email. Microsoft's post-incident report on EX1550543 is still pending, and the phishing that reuses that incident ID remains active. [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/203189/security/the-microsoft-outage-left-15-hotel-guests-poisoned-by-chlorine.html), [iTnews (phishing)](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/phishers-quick-to-exploit-extended-m365-outage-630347)
- **Expedia / n0n (developing):** there is nothing new from Expedia, which confirmed on 12 Nov that it was breached through a "compromised third-party data platform provider". n0n's claims of about **830M** loyalty rows and **333M** account records come without samples or proof, and its leak-site listing shows no deadline or ransom. Earlier reporting tied the Qubole AWS cross-account abuse to Expedia. Expedia has not named its vendor, so treat that link as probable but unconfirmed. [Skift](https://skift.com/2026/11/12/expedia-confirms-cyberattack-through-third-party-vendor/), [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/suzannerowankelleher/2026/11/13/russian-cyberattackers-hit-expedia-nearly-a-billion-traveler-records-exposed/)

## 4. Threat Watch
- **FBI Flash on Artex (follow-up):** the FBI's 13 Nov flash alert says criminal and state actors use **Artex** to automate intrusions "at machine speed", in some cases reaching privileged access in **under three hours**. Artex is an open-source AI agent framework first posted to GitHub in August by "Autumn-27". The repository is gone, but forks, archives and a commercial edition remain. Operators favour cheap Chinese models such as the DeepSeek Flash family. Agents map trust relationships and go after weaker suppliers, partner portals, mobile backends and legacy services. Censys counts more than **1,600** exposed Artex consoles. Reporting also cites Microsoft attributing Artex use in a ConnectWise supply-chain attack to Chinese state actors. Talos has published a companion analysis of AI pentest platforms. Hunt for bursts of Nmap, PsExec and WinRM activity from a single source in a short window. [The Record](https://therecord.media/artex-ai-tool-fbi-flash-alert), [Cisco Talos](https://blog.talosintelligence.com/artex-and-beyond/), [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/fbi-warns-artex-ai-hacking-tool-is-powering-global-cyberattacks/)
- **Citrus ransomware (Kaspersky):** Kaspersky describes a newly documented Russian-speaking group that attacks only Russian companies. It gets in with stolen contractor credentials over VPN or exposed RDP and escalates through the AD CS flaw "Certighost" (**CVE-2026-54121**). Its operators use LLM-written PowerShell and tunnel traffic over Xray VLESS/REALITY. They push the Go-based **Sn00py** encryptor via GPO, disguised as a Kaspersky policy update. Kaspersky links it tentatively to **Toy Ghouls**: the two share tooling such as 1C-Shell, mimikatz, PsExec and ADRecon, and in some cases had access to the same victims at the same time. The TTPs carry over to other targets: watch contractor VPN logins and AD CS certificate requests. [Securelist](https://securelist.com/citrus-ransomware/121879/), [SecurityOnline](https://securityonline.info/citrus-ransomware-sn00py-vless-llm/)
- **Tor network DDoS (ongoing):** an unidentified actor is overwhelming most Tor relays with floods of client circuits. Nobody has claimed it, and Tor has published no traffic figures. Organisations that rely on Tor for research or OSINT should expect degraded performance. [Tor Project forum](https://forum.torproject.org/t/security-release-0-4-9-17/22454)
