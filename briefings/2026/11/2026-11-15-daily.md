## 1. Risk Watch

### 1.1 Check Point Gaia CVE-2026-123039: KEV deadline Monday 16 Nov (follow-up, ONGOING)
There is nothing new from Check Point in this window, and the deadline is unchanged. **CVE-2026-123039** (CVSS **7.8**) is a local root escalation in the `dynamic_server` daemon. Its socket, `/tmp/dynamic_socket`, is world-writable and does not authenticate callers. Affected products are Gaia firewalls and management servers, Maestro and Scalable Platforms on **R81–R82.x**. In the campaign, the attackers first exploited PHP2Shell (**CVE-2026-109618/-109619**) on the Mobile Access portal, then escalated to root, installed the TSGA/TSProxy backdoor and in one wave wiped ESXi hosts. Check Point says "a couple of dozen customers" were hit. CISA added the CVE to KEV on 13 Nov, with federal remediation due **16 Nov**.
**Action:** finish rolling out the November Jumbo Hotfix to every gateway and management server before Monday. Where you cannot patch, stop `dynamic_server`. Hunt for PHP session files containing `upload_progress_pwn`, unexpected `CPconfigdaemon` processes, gaps in firewall logs, and SSH from a gateway to internal ESXi hosts.
Sources: [Security Affairs (KEV)](https://securityaffairs.com/203040/hacking/u-s-cisa-adds-check-point-and-unrealircd-flaws-to-its-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog.html), [The Hacker News (PHP2Shell chain)](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/hackers-exploit-php2shell-flaws-in.html), [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

### 1.2 Microsoft 365 outage-themed credential phishing reusing incident EX1550543 (NEW)
Microsoft Threat Intelligence reports an active campaign, detected on **13 Nov**, that imitates M365 service-health notices and cites the real Exchange Online incident **EX1550543** from the 12 Nov outage. The emails claim that messages held during the outage will be deleted within **24 hours** unless the recipient clicks a "restore" link. Subjects follow the pattern `Outage – [six-character code]`. Sender display names combine an organisation-specific prefix with "Outage Notification" or "Service Health". Links pass through recipient-specific SendGrid click tracking and then redirect to a credential-harvesting page. Microsoft calls the activity "consistent with credential phishing". It has not confirmed any stolen credentials, and it says the outage itself was not malicious.
**Action:** block the sender domains `moments-notice[.]com`, `planetmaison[.]com` and `newbooker[.]com` and the landing host `reallifeaccounting[.]com` (path `/themelist0.html`). In Defender XDR, query EmailEvents, EmailUrlInfo and UrlClickEvents for these indicators, and investigate clicks, not only deliveries. Check that Safe Links is enforced. Tell users that outage status is only ever confirmed in the M365 admin center.
Sources: [Cyber Security News (relaying @MsftSecIntel, 14 Nov)](https://cybersecuritynews.com/active-password-stealing-attack-microsoft-365/), [Security Affairs (outage background)](https://securityaffairs.com/202932/security/microsoft-cloud-outage-hits-outlook-teams-copilot-and-ai-services.html)

### 1.3 UnrealIRCd CVE-2026-114713: exploitation reported, KEV due 16 Nov (follow-up, ONGOING)
In **UnrealIRCd 6.0.0 up to but not including 6.2.7.1**, the labeled-response module does not escape the IRCv3 `label` tag when it echoes it back. On any network with two or more linked servers, an ordinary user can therefore inject server-to-server commands that run with server or IRCOp authority (CVSS **8.6**). The maintainer calls it the most critical UnrealIRCd issue in 15 years. Red Hat's tracker notes that upstream reports active exploitation attempts, which reportedly began a day after the 7 Nov hotfix. No vendor has published IOCs or a description of what the attackers are after.
**Action:** upgrade to **6.2.7.1** or apply the no-restart hot-patch bundle. If you cannot do either right away, add `blacklist-module "labeled-response";` on every linked server. Review server-to-server logs since 7 Nov for unexpected SAJOIN/KILL/SVS*-style commands from regular users.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/attackers-exploit-unrealircd-flaw-that.html), [oss-security advisory](https://seclists.org/oss-sec/2026/q4/576), [Red Hat Bugzilla](https://bugzilla.redhat.com/show_bug.cgi?id=2568818)

### 1.4 Cursor breach: Fireworks AI key → agent traces with credentials → backdoored Claude Code extension 2.1.312/2.1.313 (follow-up, new detail)
Cursor's full incident report (13 Nov) explains how the 29 Oct marketplace compromise happened. An access key that Cursor had issued to Fireworks AI was exposed through Fireworks' own breaches. With it, the attacker copied a shared, read-only bucket of mostly 2025 coding-agent traces from users who did not have Privacy Mode on. Some of those traces contained unredacted credentials. The attacker used them to reach Cursor SaaS tools, stealing archived non-production repos, support emails, credentials that users had sent to support, and usage and contact data. The attacker also used them to plant unauthorised **Anthropic.claude-code 2.1.312/2.1.313** builds in the cache of Cursor's VS Code marketplace for about **11 hours**. Those builds collected system information, installed an attacker-controlled SSH key, tried to enable SSH and set up persistence. Cursor estimates up to **5,000** users received them. Anthropic's own extension and Open VSX were not affected. Cursor says fewer than **0.01%** of customers had API credentials exposed. The last attacker activity it saw was on **1 Nov**. Wiz groups this with attacks on Railway, Northflank, Lightning AI, OpenCode, Warp and Notion under the name "Black October".
**Action:** inventory developer machines that installed the extension from Cursor's marketplace on 29 Oct. Run Cursor's remediation script, because uninstalling alone does not remove persistence. Look for unknown keys in `~/.ssh/authorized_keys` and SSH services that were newly enabled. Rotate every secret reachable from those machines. If your team received a Cursor notice, rotate the keys listed in it. More broadly, find out which of your AI-tooling vendors keep prompts or agent traces, and whether those traces can contain secrets.
Sources: [Cursor incident report](https://cursor.com/blog/security-incident-update), [Cursor extension notice](https://cursor.com/blog/claude-code-extension-security-notice), [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/cursor-confirms-security-breach-led-to-malicious-claude-code-extension/)

### 1.5 Qubole cross-account AWS role abuse reaches Expedia; extortion claim unverified (developing)
Researchers investigating the Qubole supply-chain intrusion (14–26 Oct) say the attacker took over credentials tied to Qubole's AWS account. With them, the attacker assumed idle cross-account roles that still trusted Qubole in customer accounts, then harvested secrets and Okta credentials. Expedia has now confirmed to Skift that it was breached through a "compromised third-party data platform provider", and Wiz and Sentra link the intrusion to a stolen Qubole vendor key. Expedia has not confirmed the claims of the new extortion group **n0n**: **830M+** loyalty records from 35 programmes, **58M** traveller profiles, **333M** account and credential records, and source code. Treat those figures as unverified.
**Action:** if any AWS account still has an IAM role that trusts Qubole's account, remove or restrict it with source-IP and ExternalId conditions. Review CloudTrail from **1 Oct** for `AssumeRole` calls by Qubole principals followed by Secrets Manager or SSM reads. Rotate any secrets those roles could read, including Okta and OAuth client credentials. Expedia and partner-programme customers should expect convincing booking-themed phishing.
Sources: [Security Affairs (Expedia/n0n)](https://securityaffairs.com/203062/cyber-crime/extortion-group-claims-theft-of-nearly-one-billion-expedia-traveler-records.html), [BleepingComputer (Qubole/AWS)](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-exploit-trusted-aws-access-to-breach-multiple-cloud-environments), [SecurityWeek (victim count)](https://securityweek.com/over-a-dozen-organizations-hit-in-qubole-supply-chain-attack)

## 4. Threat Watch
- **DPRK FaceStealer / BlueNoroff (follow-up):** the US has seized the cloud infrastructure behind the deepfake video-call scheme, which ran for about 18 months and stole more than **$400M**. Arctic Wolf counts **677** targets in **58** countries, mostly founders and executives, and attributes the scheme to BlueNoroff. Victims are lured from hijacked Telegram, LinkedIn, Slack and Discord accounts into meetings, recorded secretly, and then given a ClickFix-style "fix" that installs stealers for crypto wallets and Zoom, Meet and Teams credentials. The FBI also confirmed attempts against npm and Rust crate maintainers, including a thwarted compromise of a JavaScript library with tens of millions of weekly downloads. DOJ also says **$2.7B+** from the Binance theft has been frozen, with court authorisation to seize or restrain more than **$1.2B**. [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/us-disrupts-north-korean-campaign-behind-400-million-crypto-theft/), [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/us-seizes-north-korea-hacking-infrastructure-binance/)
- **AI-assisted attack infrastructure, JPCERT/CC (single source):** according to a report on 15 Nov, a JPCERT/CC-led investigation with foreign security firms has identified about **1,000** servers suspected of supporting AI-driven attacks since October, of which it is tracking **600–700**. Most hosted AI tooling such as the **Artex** framework, and one held traces of an Anthropic coding tool. The servers were used for vulnerability scanning, attempts to steal configuration files, and API-based intrusion attempts. No JPCERT/CC publication has been found yet, so verify against JPCERT/CC before quoting the figures. This follows the FBI's FLASH-20261113-001 on Artex. [The Business Standard](https://www.tbsnews.net/worldbiz/asia/japan-led-probe-finds-1000-servers-suspected-supporting-ai-driven-cyberattacks-1602766), [The Record (FBI FLASH)](https://therecord.media/artex-ai-tool-fbi-flash-alert)
- **BlackNevas ransomware on ESXi (secondary coverage):** reports on 14 Nov describe BlackNevas, a Trigona derivative first seen in November 2024, wiping VMs and backup infrastructure on ESXi. Earlier summaries of a Kaspersky GERT case say the attacker got there by compromising a PRTG monitoring server that had broad access to the ESXi hosts. The primary report could not be checked. Look for unusual root SSH or API access from monitoring and management servers to hypervisors. [Gridinsoft (summary)](https://blog.gridinsoft.com/esxi-proxmox-ransomware-root-access/), [SentinelOne (family profile)](https://www.sentinelone.com/anthology/blacknevas/)
