## 1. Risk Watch

### 1.1 WordPress core CVE-2026-119977 added to CISA KEV, federal deadline 6 Nov (NEW)
**The flaw:** during full-package core upgrades, WordPress unpacks files, including the installer, into a predictable, web-accessible directory under `wp-content/upgrade`.
- **Attack:** an unauthenticated attacker who requests the staged installer during the brief upgrade window can reach PHP code execution. It is a time-of-check/time-of-use race.
- **Severity:** Wordfence scores it **CVSS 8.1**; WordPress (advisory GHSA-4w3m-c66w-69mg) calls it low severity because it is hard to win.
- **Affected and fixed:** **4.7 through 7.1.3**, fixed in **7.1.4** (28 Oct). Backports to branches 4.7+ are rolling out.

**Exploitation:**
- **Wordfence:** blocked **62,969** attack attempts in 24 hours after the patch. Some sources polled small groups of sites for about 14.5 hours, waiting for an upgrade to start.
- **F5 Labs:** reports more than 18,500 probing requests but no confirmed compromises.
- **CISA:** listed it on **3 Nov** (catalog 2026.11.03) with a due date of **6 Nov**.

Action:
- Update to 7.1.4 or the patched backport for your branch now. Managed hosts should confirm that auto-update has completed.
- Block web access to PHP files under `wp-content/upgrade/` at the web server or WAF.
- Hunt for requests to `wp-content/upgrade/*/setup-config.php` or `install.php`, unexpected administrator accounts, and new plugins or PHP files created around recent upgrade times.

Sources: [CISA alert](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/11/03/cisa-adds-one-known-exploited-vulnerability-catalog) · [WordPress 7.1.4](https://wordpress.org/news/2026/10/wordpress-7-1-4/) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/wordpress-rce-flaw-draws-nearly-63000.html) · [F5 Labs](https://www.f5.com/labs/articles/weekly-threat-bulletin-november-4th-2026)

### 1.2 cPGuard update channel hijacked to backdoor Linux hosting servers (NEW)
**What happened:** at **02:06 UTC on 2 Nov**, an attacker used compromised credentials to reach OPSSHIELD's update CDN. They pushed a malicious script inside cPGuard updates **5.88.12 to 5.88.19** (cPGuard is a security add-on for cPanel and other hosting servers).
- **What the script did:**
  - It installed a backdoor shell.
  - It collected `/etc/shadow`, `/etc/passwd`, root's MySQL/MariaDB `my.cnf`, root's shell history, and root's `authorized_keys` and `known_hosts`.
  - It uploaded the collected files to OPSSHIELD's own licensing server through a compromised account.
- **Data taken:** the attacker retrieved **32** of those files, one per customer server. OPSSHIELD stresses this is a preliminary count, not the total number of affected servers.
- **Fix:** **5.88.25+**. OPSSHIELD has also shipped an update that removes its own support-access keys from root's `authorized_keys`.
- **Unknowns:** how the credentials were obtained, and whether the private support key was exposed.

Action:
- Run `grep 'Successfully updated' /opt/cpguard/logs/install_log`. Any entry for 5.88.12–5.88.19 means the server should be treated as compromised, even if it now runs a fixed build.
- Update with `cpgcli update --start` and confirm the version with `cpgcli --version`.
- Look for `cpguard-v58812-i91511.sh`, `/var/tmp/.cpg91511_sh`, `/var/tmp/.cpg91511/` and `loot.tgz`. Their absence does not prove the server is clean, because the fixed update removes them.
- On any exposed server, reset root and all local passwords from a trusted environment, rotate database passwords and S3/DigitalOcean tokens and any secrets in root's history, and replace `/root/.ssh` keys and remove their public halves from systems that trust them.

Sources: [OPSSHIELD advisory](https://opsshield.com/notice/cpguard-update-channel-compromise.html) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/cpguard-update-channel-hijacked-to.html)

### 1.3 Refrigeration sabotage wave 2 spreads to nine EU countries and the UK (DEVELOPING)
**Scope (overnight 1–2 Nov):**
- More than **100** stores were hit in Belgium, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Sweden, Ireland and Estonia, with further incidents in Germany, Italy, Portugal and Finland.
- **Ireland:** SuperValu pulled chilled and frozen stock from **13** stores.
- **Portugal:** seven Intermarché stores.
- **Italy:** Coop stores in Sicily.
- **UK:** three meat suppliers that belong to the Association of Independent Meat Suppliers (AIMS), via **Copeland XWEB** controllers whose fix has been available since August, and a produce wholesaler. The NCSC says national impact is "minimal" so far and has been contacting owners of exposed systems.

**How:** attackers forced evaporators into defrost mode through internet-exposed supervisory interfaces. Lupovis saw login attempts with Danfoss's published factory-default credentials. Kevin Beaumont says every affected unit had an internet-reachable management interface, and many are end-of-life.

**Attribution:** SentinelLABS's assessment finds no established actor. Russian-aligned hacktivists (CARR, NoName057(16), Z-Pentest) are a credible hypothesis but unproven, and a Z-Pentest claim about a Spanish store is unverified. Suspected Iranian involvement applies only to the US commissary cases and is also unverified. Belgium's federal prosecutor has opened an investigation.

Action:
- Inventory every refrigeration and building-management controller, including those installed by contractors (Danfoss AK-SM/AK-SC/StoreView, Copeland/Dixell XWEB), and take them off public IPs. Allow access only through a VPN.
- Change default credentials and update firmware (StoreView **4.3.1+**; current XWEB firmware).
- Preserve logs and configurations before resetting a unit, and review defrost ranges.
- Alert on setpoint or defrost changes outside maintenance windows.

Sources: [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/over-100-european-stores-hit-as-supermarket-fridge-attacks-resume/) · [The Record](https://therecord.media/british-businesses-caught-in-europe-wide-supermarket-fridge-attacks) · [SentinelLABS](https://www.sentinelone.com/labs/defrost-and-disrupt-an-assessment-of-europe-s-refrigeration-attacks/) · [heise](https://www.heise.de/news/Sabotage-an-Kuehlanlagen-Experte-ruegt-Hersteller-11510792.html)

### 1.4 Linux kernel CVE-2026-98432: Ubuntu fixes reach `-proposed` (follow-up)
**Ubuntu status:** fixed kernels for this remote IPv4 TCP use-after-free (**CVSS 9.8**) are in the opt-in `-proposed` pocket, which does not install automatically:
- **22.04:** `5.15.0-203.213`
- **24.04:** `6.8.0-149.149`
- **26.04:** `7.0.0-43.43`
- **26.10:** `7.3.0-10.10`

The CVE page (updated **3 Nov**) still lists no USN. 16.04–20.04 are vulnerable with no fix in progress (Ubuntu Pro ESM only), and cloud, FIPS and real-time variants are still work in progress.

**Other vendors:** Red Hat fixed RHEL 8, 9 and 10 on 1 Nov (RHSA-2026:93412/93413/93414). WatchGuard published **WGSA-2026-0033** on 3 Nov for its own products.

**Exploitation:** none reported in the wild. Only proof-of-concept code that triggers the race (not code execution) is public, and it is not in KEV.

Action:
- Patch RHEL now.
- For Ubuntu, test the `-proposed` kernels on your highest-exposure internet-facing hosts and plan a reboot window for when the USN lands. Check with `uname -r`.
- Keep the interim mitigations (drop inbound IPv4 packets with IP options; minimise exposed TCP listeners) in place until then.

Sources: [Ubuntu CVE tracker](https://ubuntu.com/security/CVE-2026-98432) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/red-hat-patches-98-rated-linux-tcp-rce.html) · [WatchGuard WGSA-2026-0033](https://www.watchguard.com/WGSA-2026-0033/)

### 1.5 Binance root cause: trojanized installer, fake job interview and an abused internal AI tool (follow-up)
**Binance's 3 Nov disclosure of the $6.43B theft describes a five-month chain:**
- **June:** a Wallet DevOps engineer downloaded a trojanized Homebrew installer while searching for a popular AI tool. Binance rotated credentials, but details of the team's roles had already leaked.
- **September:** a second engineer was approached on a job platform and taken through fake HR and "CEO" interviews. They were then told to run an "assignment" repository that installed a poisoned **pyenv** component and wiped forensic traces.
- **20 Oct:** the attacker used that machine to abuse an internal AI tool and gain control of two colleagues' computers, one of which held a container-deployment token.
- **29 Oct (17:15–19:56):** the attacker deployed a container that called the internal wallet-signing service directly, outside the normal withdrawal flow.

**Status:** about **$2.85B** has been frozen with partners. The FBI attributes the theft to North Korea's **TraderTraitor**, which Pyongyang denies.

Action:
- Treat developer workstations as tier-0 if they can reach signing or deployment systems.
- Hunt for Homebrew or pyenv binaries that do not match the official releases, and for "take-home assignment" repositories cloned onto corporate machines.
- Restrict what internal AI or agent tools can execute on other users' endpoints.
- Require hardware-backed approval for any call to signing services that bypasses the normal workflow.

Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/binance-reveals-how-fake-job-interviews.html) · [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/03/north-korean-fake-job-interview-led-to-63b-binance-heist/5307398) · [The Record](https://therecord.media/binance-cryptocurrency-theft-months-long-campaign-nkorea)
