## 1. Risk Watch

### 1.1 OpenSSH "SSHole" CVE-2026-123158 now exploited in the wild (follow-up, ONGOING)
- **What changed:** Previdian saw the first exploitation attempt **less than nine hours** after the CVE was published. The attacker got **root on a honeypot** running real Alpine Linux and OpenSSH. Previdian counted **8 attempts**, all from one Japan-geolocated IP (**89.116.89[.]18**). Each sent an oversized password and then ran `id`, `uname` and `hostname` over non-interactive SSH.
- **Caveats:** this comes from a single vendor and a single sensor. CISA has not added the flaw to KEV; its CVE enrichment rates exploitation as "poc".
- **The bug (recap):** on musl builds without PAM, any password of **257–1,024 bytes** logs in to an account whose `/etc/shadow` hash is `*`. **CVSS 8.1**, fixed in **OpenSSH 10.6p2**.
- **Exposure:** Alpine's default openssh-server, and possibly OpenWrt, Buildroot and Yocto builds and many musl-based Docker images. glibc distributions, PAM-based password checks and Dropbear are not affected.

**Action:** upgrade to OpenSSH 10.6p2 or your distribution's fixed package (Alpine backports go down to 3.16) and restart sshd. Where you can't patch yet, set `PasswordAuthentication no`. Inventory musl images and appliances for accounts with `*` hashes, including the `alpine` user on cloud images. Hunt auth logs for successful password logins to key-only or passwordless accounts, and for traffic from 89.116.89[.]18.

**Sources:** [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/06/openssh-sshole-cve-2026-123158-exploitation/) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/hackers-exploited-openssh-sshole-flaw.html) · [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/openssh-rushes-patches-for-sshole-authentication-bypass/) · [DataBreaches.net](https://databreaches.net/2026/11/06/any-long-password-will-do-ssh-flaw-lets-hackers-into-alpine-systems-routers-and-more/)

### 1.2 SonicWall SonicOS SSL-VPN SAML/MFA bypass CVE-2026-19868 exploited, added to KEV (NEW)
- **The flaw:** an attacker with valid credentials for an alternate authentication method can bypass SAML authentication, which may include SAML-enforced MFA. **CVSS 7.1**. Only SSL-VPN configured for SAML is affected.
- **Exploitation:** SonicWall PSIRT says it investigated a report of possible exploitation. CISA added the CVE to KEV on **6 Nov**, with a **20 Nov** deadline.
- **Same release:** six more SonicOS flaws are fixed, including two management-interface bugs rated **8.8** that let unauthenticated attackers perform unauthorized operations, pre-authentication overflows in SSL-VPN and TACACS+ that can cause denial of service, and weak password hashing on virtual appliances.
- **Versions:** affects Gen7 **7.3.3-7015** and earlier and Gen8 **8.2.2-8015** and earlier. Fixed in **7.3.4-7013** and **8.2.3-8015**. There are no workarounds.
- **Context:** this follows months of attacks on SonicWall SMA1000 appliances.

**Action:** upgrade SonicOS now. Restrict the management interface to admin networks. Review SSL-VPN logs since October for sessions that skipped your SAML IdP: look for logins that succeeded with local or LDAP credentials when SAML was required. Rotate those accounts' credentials.

**Sources:** [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/sonicwall-warns-of-new-ssl-vpn-mfa-bypass-flaw-exploited-in-attacks/) · [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

### 1.3 Barracuda ESG pre-authentication remote root: full chain now public (follow-up)
- **What changed:** watchTowr published a patch-diff analysis of 9.4.0.027 against 9.4.0.029. It walks through how **CVE-2026-116606** (pre-authentication authorization bypass, **CVSS 9.3**) chains with **CVE-2026-111560** (OS command injection, **CVSS 8.6**) to give remote root on the Email Security Gateway.
- **Wider exposure:** CVE-2026-111560 also affects Load Balancer ADC, Message Archiver, WAF Control Center, Web Application Firewall and Web Security Gateway.
- **Why it matters:** Barracuda fixed both on 4 Nov. Public write-ups of edge-device chains are usually followed by mass exploitation within days. UNC4841's 2023 ESG campaign forced customers to physically replace appliances.

**Action:** confirm every ESG and affected Barracuda appliance is at **9.4.0.029** or has the published patches. Where patching lagged past 5 Nov, hunt for unexpected processes, cron entries or files on the appliance, and for outbound connections from it.

**Sources:** [watchTowr Labs](https://labs.watchtowr.com/barracuda-networks-is-your-new-best-friend-barracuda-email-security-gateway-pre-auth-remote-root-rce-cve-2026-116606-cve-2026-111560/) · [Barracuda advisory](https://trust.barracuda.com/security/pre-authentication-authorization-bypass-command-injection)

### 1.4 ARTEX abuse spreads to the financial sector: Microsoft names Storm-3358 (ONGOING)
- **The actor:** Microsoft Threat Intelligence describes **Storm-3358**, a financially motivated cluster it places in Cambodia. It mainly targets financial organizations in Southeast Asia, plus some large global firms.
- **Technique:** it uses the ARTEX agentic framework to scan internet-facing systems and exploit anonymous file upload and SSRF. It then plants web shells, sprays passwords, enumerates APIs, dumps databases and abuses cloud identities, moving laterally at machine speed.
- **Tooling:** WISP, vshell and AdaptixC2 RATs, fetched with curl and netcat.
- **Timeframe:** activity seen **19 Oct–4 Nov** and still active on 6 Nov.
- **Wider picture:** Censys says ARTEX use goes well beyond the campaigns already reported.
- **WorkOS (correction to earlier reporting):** WorkOS now says a security researcher was behind its ARTEX-driven breach. The researcher says their agent "escaped" its test environment and hit WorkOS unintentionally. The agent got in through an exposed legacy API that accepted a predictable development credential and had no rate limiting.

**Action:** hunt the past 30 days for **199.19.95[.]66–68**, **91.92.240[.]238**, **85.11.168[.]45**, **85.11.167[.]52**, **31.77.229[.]35** and **178.16.54[.]129**. Microsoft's own query lists 178.15.54.129, so verify that one before blocking. Also hunt for curl downloads of new binaries on web servers. Enforce Entra ID risk-based Conditional Access. Look for development credentials and unthrottled APIs on legacy internal tools.

**Sources:** [Microsoft Threat Intelligence](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/06/artex-abuse-expands-as-cybercriminals-target-financial-sector/) · [Infosecurity Magazine (Censys)](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/artex-activity-exceeds-reported/) · [BleepingComputer (WorkOS)](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/runaway-ai-hacking-agent-behind-workos-breach-after-escaping-sandbox/)

### 1.5 QNAP NAS compromised through Plex2Shell; D-Link DGS-3130 zero-days with no fix (NEW)
- **QNAP and Plex:** QNAP says users report Plex Media Server on their NAS compromised through the **Plex2Shell** chain (**CVE-2026-96652, -96654, -96655, -96656**). The chain has been in KEV since 20 Oct. QTS and QuTS hero themselves are not affected.
  - QNAP says to treat an internet-exposed, vulnerable Plex server and **all NAS content as compromised**. Updating to Plex **1.43.3.10861** does not remove payloads already installed.
- **D-Link DGS-3130:** D-Link disclosed several unpatched flaws in the DGS-3130-30TS switches' D-Link Discovery Protocol (DDP) and firmware security. They allow leaks of device data and SNMP communities, unauthorized configuration changes and device resets. There are no CVE IDs or fixed firmware yet.

**Action (QNAP):** check the indicators in QNAP's bulletin. If compromised, clean-reinstall QTS or QuTS hero and reset Plex and NAS credentials. Stop forwarding port **32400** and put remote access behind a VPN.

**Action (D-Link):** disable DDP, remove default or unneeded SNMP communities, limit SSH, web and SNMP to an isolated management network, and watch for unexpected configuration changes or reboots.

**Sources:** [BleepingComputer (QNAP)](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/qnap-says-hackers-breaching-nas-devices-in-plex2shell-attacks/) · [BleepingComputer (D-Link)](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/d-link-warns-of-multiple-zero-days-in-dgs-3130-series-switches/)
