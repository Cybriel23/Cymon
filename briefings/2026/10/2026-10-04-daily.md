## 1. Risk Watch

### 1.1 NetScaler build 14.1-73.37 reboots in loops on SAML traffic — the September patch is now the outage (NEW, ONGOING)

Administrators who applied Citrix's emergency fix for the actively exploited NetScaler zero-days are reporting that **internet-facing appliances running 14.1-73.37 entered forced reboot cycles**. The mechanism, as Citrix describes it, is **crafted SAML authentication traffic that crashes the `nsaaad` service**; repeated authentication-process failure trips the watchdog, which restarts the device. Citrix says it is "tracking a newly seen SAML issue" and will publish corrective guidance, but as of this morning there is **no new CVE, no published root cause and no replacement build number**. Citrix is explicit on the one question that matters most: **the reboot reports do not demonstrate that attackers have bypassed the September patch** — current evidence points to a patch side-effect rather than a security regression. Hold both halves of that in mind. If you are on **14.1-73.37** with SAML authentication configured on a public-facing gateway, you have a remotely triggerable availability problem on the box your workforce uses for remote access, and no fix to apply. If you have not patched, you still have **CVE-2026-88771/88772** — **KEV deadline expired 30 September**, roughly **50,000 devices possibly still exposed** — and rolling back is not a defensible answer either. **Action:** do not roll back to an unpatched build. Instead, confirm which build each node actually runs (mixed-build HA pairs are how this gets misdiagnosed), **correlate reboot timestamps against SAML authentication volume** to establish whether you are seeing this issue or something else, and preserve **crash dumps and `nsaaad` core files before the box reboots again** — they are the evidence Citrix support will need and they are also what distinguishes a crash bug from an exploitation attempt. Open or keep open a support case so you are in the queue for the corrected build, and if your architecture allows it, **front SAML authentication with an upstream filter or move affected gateways to an alternative authentication path temporarily**. Separately — and this is the step most teams still have not completed — patching never resolved the September intrusions: the **`sec_monitor` superuser written into `/flash/nsconfig/ns.conf` survives upgrade**. Re-run the triage: diff `ns.conf` for unexpected superusers, check **`/var/netscaler/logon/LogonPoint/.local_journal`** and **`.ctxs.receiver`**, diff **`/etc/httpd.conf`** for CSS-like paths mapped to PHP, verify **`/bin/sh`** is not mode **6555**, hunt **64.94.85[.]67**, **45.141.21[.]130**, **31.56.197[.]72:9090** and **23.27.143[.]20:9000**, and rotate everything the appliance brokers per **CTX694799**. **Sources:** [Cyber Security News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/citrix-netscaler-0-day-patch/) · [GBHackers](https://gbhackers.com/citrix-netscaler-appliances-reboot/) · [Security Online](https://securityonline.info/netscaler-saml-authentication-issue/) · [Unit 42](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/netscaler-zero-days-exploited/)

### 1.2 FortiMail CVE-2026-104286: KEV deadline is today and there is still no patch (follow-up, ONGOING, EXPLOITED)

Re-verified at the primary source this morning, three days after the KEV listing: **Fortinet's FG-IR-26-175 still publishes no fixed build.** Every solution field reads "upgrade to **upcoming**" — **8.0.2 or above** for the 8.0 train, **7.6.7 or above** for 7.6, **7.4.9 or above** for 7.4 — and **7.2.x** is told to migrate to the **7.4 branch**, which is itself unpatched. The flaw is unchanged: **CVSS 9.8**, **path traversal (CWE-22)** combined with **improper neutralisation of NULL bytes (CWE-158)**, letting an **unauthenticated** attacker **write arbitrary files** via crafted HTTP/HTTPS requests to the IBE endpoint; found internally by **Gwendal Guégniaud** of Fortinet Product Security; **exploited in the wild**. **Affected: 8.0.0–8.0.1, 7.6.0–7.6.6, 7.4.0–7.4.8, 7.2.0–7.2.9** — in practice every supported version. **CISA's due date is today, 4 October.** Neither Fortinet nor CISA has published exploitation scale, victim count, first-exploitation date or attribution. **Action:** the workaround is your remediation and should be in place before the day is out. Fortinet now documents three mitigations, and you should apply all that fit: **disable IBE** (`config system encryption ibe` / `set status disable`), **restrict webmail and management access to trusted networks**, and **configure your WAF to block POST requests to `/ibe` containing `../`** — the last is the cheapest compensating control if you cannot drop IBE for business reasons. Then hunt, because the published indicators describe full device persistence: **`/data/etc/ld.so.preload`** referencing an unattributable **`/data/lib/liblog.so`**, replaced **`/data/bin/webconsole`** and **`/data/bin/mailservice`**, modified **`/bin/smit`** and **`/data/etc/httpd.conf`**, a dropped **`/data/migadmin.tar.gz`**, cron entries referencing **`/migadmin`**, and an **`archive234`** account. The log artefact of exploitation is **IBE decryption errors containing invalid Base64 data**. Block and hunt **79.141.169[.]187** and **45.129.0[.]192**. **Capture device state before you reboot or reimage** — an `ld.so.preload` implant vanishes with a clean reinstall done before evidence collection. Any finding means exposure of **mail content, routing configuration and every credential the appliance stores or brokers**; rotate accordingly. For the compliance record, document the workaround as your **BOD 26-04** mitigation — that is what CISA's required-action language contemplates when no vendor update exists. **Sources:** [Fortinet FG-IR-26-175](https://www.fortiguard.com/psirt/FG-IR-26-175) · [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog) · [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/exploited-fortinet-fortimail-zero-day-calls-for-urgent-action/) · [watchTowr FAQ](https://watchtowr.com/intelligence/fortinet-fortimail-cve-2026-104286-faq/)

### 1.3 Zammad CVE-2026-102489 / CVE-2026-102490: deadline tomorrow, half the chain still unfixed (follow-up, EXPLOITED)

No change in 24 hours, which is itself the finding: **both CVEs remain in KEV with a due date of tomorrow, 5 October**, and Zammad's position — published on its community forum and linked from the KEV entry — still stands unamended. The vendor states it has **not received technical details about CVE-2026-102490 from DIVD**, **cannot verify** the claim that it affects nearly every version ever released, and has **no fix available**, while recommending administrators move to **7.2.0** (released **23 September**, release notes silent on both CVEs). CISA's own naming is the more precise description: **CVE-2026-102489** is **session fixation** leading to **remote code execution as the `zammad` user**, **CVE-2026-102490** is **improper privilege management** allowing that local user to **escalate to root**, and CISA notes each can be chained with the other. Version data resolves the chain as half-mitigated: **CVE-2026-102489 affects 6.3.0–6.5.4** and is present-but-not-exploitable in **7.0.0–7.1.3** for environmental reasons, while **CVE-2026-102490 spans v1.5.0 through the v7.1.0 alpha**. Exploitation context is the **21 September** intrusion at **DIVD**, where an agentic attacker ran **password spraying and a man-in-the-middle attack simultaneously** and completed the chain "in seconds," pivoting to other services and exfiltrating data, limited by **network segmentation**. **Action:** treat **7.2.0** as the entry-point fix and nothing more. Get any internet-facing instance behind authentication or offline today, upgrade to **7.2.0**, and until the LPE is fixed operate the host on the assumption that **any code execution on it equals root** — isolate it, strip stored credentials and API tokens for integrated systems off it, and keep it outside shared trust boundaries. Preserve application and authentication logs **before** remediating, and run **DIVD's `cve-2026-102489_ioc_check_script_v2.sh`**. Because a helpdesk holds ticket attachments, customer correspondence and whatever credentials users paste into tickets, scope any finding as a **data-exposure incident**, not merely a server compromise. **Sources:** [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog) · [Zammad community advisory](https://community.zammad.org/t/take-care-local-privilege-escalation-cve-2026-102490-is-reported-as-being-actively-exploited/21297/2) · [DIVD-2026-00015](https://csirt.divd.nl/cases/DIVD-2026-00015/) · [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/200248/security/u-s-cisa-adds-zammad-gmbh-zammad-flaws-to-its-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog.html)

### 1.4 Warlock is exploiting six SharePoint CVEs beyond ToolShell — your remediation scope is probably wrong (NEW, ONGOING)

**Symantec and the Carbon Black Threat Hunter Team** have published an expanded picture of **Warlock** (also **Gold Salem**, **Longlegs**, **Storm-2603**), the suspected China-linked group that has been farming SharePoint since mid-2025. The detail that should change your work today is the CVE list. Alongside the **ToolShell** flaws (**CVE-2025-49704**, **CVE-2025-49706**, **CVE-2025-53770**, **CVE-2025-53771**), Symantec names six further weaponised SharePoint vulnerabilities: **CVE-2026-32201**, **CVE-2026-45659**, **CVE-2026-56164**, **CVE-2026-58644**, **CVE-2026-50522** and **CVE-2026-55040**. Symantec does **not** report a new unpatched zero-day — these are known, patched flaws — but almost every SharePoint remediation programme built in 2025 was scoped to the four ToolShell CVEs, and that scope is now demonstrably insufficient. The tradecraft is otherwise consistent and fast: web shells tailored to multiple SharePoint versions, extraction of **ASP.NET machine keys** to forge valid payloads, **RCE inside the SharePoint application pool**, **DLL sideloading** and living-off-the-land reconnaissance, **BYOVD abuse of the K7RKScan.sys driver (CVE-2025-1055)** to disable endpoint protection, payload delivery through legitimate cloud services, **VS Code tunnels** for remote access, and **ransomware staged in SYSVOL** for domain-wide distribution. The tempo figure is the one to quote to leadership: in one intrusion the actor **pushed its security-tool-disabling utility to at least 40 hosts in about two hours, then deployed Warlock on at least 33**. Confirmed victims in the past two months are **four organisations** — **two critical infrastructure operators (a water utility and a telecommunications provider), a regional government body and a university** — in **Portuguese- and Spanish-speaking countries across Europe, Africa and Latin America**. Symantec published **no IOCs** with this report. **Action:** re-scope your SharePoint patch verification against the full ten-CVE set above, not the ToolShell four, and treat any on-premises SharePoint farm that has ever been exposed as needing **ASP.NET machine key rotation** — patching does not invalidate keys an attacker already has. Hunt unexpected **`.aspx` in `LAYOUTS`**, **`code-insiders.exe` or VS Code tunnel processes on servers**, **unexpected SYSVOL writes**, and **K7RKScan.sys loading on hosts with no K7 product installed** — that last one is a high-fidelity BYOVD signal and belongs in a blocklist, not just a detection. Legacy C2 to hunt: **litter[.]catbox[.]moe**, **xn8xyt-drop[.]s3[.]wasabisys[.]com**. **Sources:** [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/warlock-expands-sharepoint-exploitation-in-critical-infrastructure-attacks/) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/warlock-exploits-sharepoint-flaws-to.html)

### 1.5 Fortra Core Privileged Access Manager (BoKS): authentication bypass from timestamp-seeded passwords, CVSS 9.9 (NEW)

**Fortra** patched **eight vulnerabilities** in **Core Privileged Access Manager (BoKS)**, the product many organisations use to centrally govern root and privileged access across Unix and Linux fleets — so a compromise here is not one server, it is your entire privileged-access control plane. **Three are critical.** **CVE-2026-79901** (**CVSS 9.9**) is an **authentication bypass** whose root cause deserves to be read carefully: Active Directory service-account passwords were generated from **a predictable pseudo-random sequence seeded with the current Unix timestamp**, so an attacker who knows the service principal and can estimate when a password was rotated can **reproduce candidate passwords and verify them offline** — no network interaction with the target during the guessing phase, and therefore no failed-authentication telemetry to alert on. **CVE-2026-12627** (**CVSS 9.8**) is a **stack buffer overflow in autoregistration**, reachable by a **remote unauthenticated** attacker to trigger memory corruption. **CVE-2026-79898** (**CVSS 9.1**) is **command injection in `crlserver`**, allowing an authenticated user to substitute shell commands that **execute as root on the BoKS Master**, exploitable over the network through the **BCC and WSI REST or SOAP APIs**. The five remaining high- and medium-severity fixes cover heap overflows, out-of-bounds reads, insecure temporary files and further predictable-password-generation defects. **Fortra reports no evidence of exploitation in the wild.** **Action:** patch per Fortra's product security advisory, and recognise that **patching alone does not remediate CVE-2026-79901** — any Active Directory service-account password that BoKS generated under the flawed algorithm must be treated as **derivable and therefore compromised**, so **rotate them after patching**, in that order. Restrict the **BCC and WSI API endpoints** to management networks, confirm the **BoKS Master is not reachable from general user subnets**, and review authentication logs for **successful service-account logons from unexpected sources**, which is the only trace the offline-guessing path leaves behind. **Sources:** [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/fortra-patches-critical-vulnerabilities-in-boks/)

## 3. Incident Watch

- **DTU: Denmark's technical university loses CPR numbers and next-of-kin details on up to 200,000 people from its IAM system (NEW).** Attackers used **compromised credentials** to access **DTUBasen**, the university's identity and access management platform, and downloaded **personal data spanning more than two decades**. The platform holds records for roughly **40,000 active users and 160,000 former users** — hence the **up to 200,000** figure, though DTU is candid that it **cannot determine precisely what was downloaded or how many people are affected**. Exposed fields for current users: **Danish civil registration (CPR) numbers**, names, **home addresses**, profile pictures, work emails, job titles, office locations, and **next-of-kin names, relationships and phone numbers**; former users hold similar data, though addresses and next-of-kin details are auto-deleted after six months. - **"Cling": an IoT botnet that hides its C2 inside STUN traffic and spoofs Google's STUN server (NEW).** **Nozomi Networks Labs** documented **Cling**, a **MIPS** botnet whose command channel is disguised as **STUN (Session Traversal Utilities for NAT)** — the same protocol your WebRTC, Teams, Zoom and Webex traffic legitimately uses, which is precisely why it passes. Entry is via **CVE-2021-35394**, the **Realtek Jungle SDK** RCE (**2.0–3.4.14B**), with UDP packets beginning **`orf;`** followed by shell commands, using **BusyBox wget** to pull binaries tagged **`realtek.selfrep`**; further embedded exploits target **LB-LINK routers, TBK DVRs, Linksys, Eir, FiberHome** and **MVPower CCTV DVRs**.
