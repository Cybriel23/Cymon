## 1. Risk Watch

### 1.1 Ivanti Connect Secure chain, CVE-2026-104935 (ONGOING, follow-up)
Volexity attributes the chain to **UTA0576**: an authentication bypass and RCE (CVE-2026-104935) run code as the cgi-server user, and a third flaw in the privileged dsinvoked service and its DSCache configuration store gives root, even on a fully patched appliance. Fixes for the first two are in **22.8R2.5, 25.1.1.2, 25.1.3.0 and 25.1.3.1**. The escalation flaw reportedly has **no CVE and no fix**; one source says a fix is still weeks away. The chain modifies code only in memory, so Ivanti's Integrity Checker Tool does not detect it. Earliest activity is dated **1 Oct** (Defused and another researcher) or **3 Oct** (Volexity), possibly late September. Ivanti advises moving to the 25.x branch because 22.x is nearing end of life. I found no new release since 14 Oct.
**Action:** Upgrade to a fixed build, remove internet exposure of portal and management interfaces where possible, treat pre-patch exposure as potential compromise (capture memory and network forensics, rotate credentials and certificates held on the device), and watch Ivanti's advisory page for the escalation fix.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/attacker-chains-three-ivanti-connect.html) · [BleepingComputer](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/ivanti-warns-of-connect-secure-zero-day-exploited-in-attacks) · [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/security/ivanti-zero-day-july-cisa-patch-no-fix/) · [SOCRadar](https://socradar.io/blog/cve-2026-104935-ivanti-connect-secure/)

### 1.2 French supermarket refrigeration attacks, Danfoss controllers (DEVELOPING, follow-up)
An overnight attack on **11 to 12 Oct** remotely forced defrost cycles and pushed temperatures to about **50 °C**. French government figures cite about **700** supermarkets; reporting names 300 Intermarché/Netto, more than 100 Coopérative U and 80 Carrefour stores. Danfoss says most affected equipment is legacy and no longer receives security updates; its **AK-SM800** and **AK-SC355** controllers were named, and one AK-SM800 ran outdated firmware. Claroty reported flaws in these controllers in August, which Danfoss and Copeland say are since fixed. Reuters counted at least **4,500** Danfoss interfaces on Shodan. The Paris prosecutor's anti-cybercrime unit leads the inquiry and ANSSI is talking to European counterparts. Two Alsace stores reportedly lost about €270,000. I found no attribution, claim, ransom demand or arrest, and the intrusion method is not established.
**Action:** Find internet-reachable refrigeration and building-management controllers, put them behind VPN or allow-lists, isolate end-of-life units, change default or shared credentials and review logs for unexpected defrost or setpoint changes.
Sources: [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/security/danfoss-refrigeration-hack-france-supermarkets/) · [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/?p=200863) · [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/europe/french-supermarkets-refrigerator-shutdown-cyberattack)

### 1.3 IDCF Cloud (SoftBank), Japan (ONGOING, follow-up)
The attack of **7 Oct** on East Japan Region 1 hit about **495** organisations including prefectural sites, a news agency, a cold-chain logistics operator and JR East's email service. Virtual servers in four availability zones stopped and cannot restart; IDC Frontier warned that data retrieval would be difficult and told customers to rebuild in a separate environment from their own backups. Management consoles are disabled in all regions pending security verification, and staff restore servers manually on request. Entry route and actor are undisclosed. The attackers' claims (3.6 PB, **554,153** snapshots deleted) are unconfirmed, as is a figure of up to 6.09 million JR East email-address records.
**Action:** IDCF customers: rebuild from offline backups and keep backups and hypervisor credentials outside the provider's management plane. Others: verify that backups are not reachable from your cloud provider's control plane.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/ransomware-attack-disrupts-japans-idcf-cloud-used-by-govt-clients/) · [SDxCentral](https://www.sdxcentral.com/news/softbank-national-cloud-arm-attacked-in-seven-minutes-taking-japan-infrastructure-offline/)

### 1.4 Windows Shell Mark-of-the-Web bypass, CVE-2026-96951 (ONGOING, unverified detail)
BleepingComputer reports the October Patch Tuesday contained one actively exploited zero-day and two publicly disclosed ones; the exploited flaw is a Windows Shell security feature bypass defeating Mark of the Web. Security Affairs reports CISA added it to KEV on **15 Oct**; a single secondary source gives CVSS **8.8**, user interaction required (phishing delivery likely) and 13 Oct as the KEV date. No actor or exploit chain is named. I could not load Microsoft's or CISA's entries, and patch-count totals differ by tracker (1,411 vs 1,415).
**Action:** Confirm the October cumulative update is deployed, prioritising endpoints that open email attachments and downloads; check the KEV due date; do not rely on Mark of the Web alone to gate downloaded files.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/microsoft/microsoft-october-2026-patch-tuesday-fixes-1-411-flaws-3-zero-days/) · [Penligent](https://www.penligent.ai/hackinglabs/?p=7785)
