# Daily Cyber Threat Intelligence Briefing
Sunday, 11 October 2026 · Coverage: past 24 hours and follow-ups on tracked topics

## Executive Summary
Weekend tempo is moderate, but the edge-device exploitation wave that began in late September is still the dominant operational concern. Four items matter most today. (1) **Fortinet FortiMail CVE-2026-104286** (CVSS **9.8**, unauthenticated arbitrary file write) is exploited in the wild, and patch availability is still unclear. (2) **Citrix NetScaler CVE-2026-88771 / -88772** (RCE zero-days, CVSS **9.5**) plus the DoS flaw **CVE-2026-88779** are being exploited against roughly **22,000** exposed appliances. (3) **Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager CVE-2026-76504** (CVSS **9.8** auth bypass) is on KEV with no workaround. (4) The **Bitget $387.5M theft**, attributed to DPRK actors who used zero-days in two third-party security appliances, is a reminder that security appliances are the entry point. Two caveats on this edition: the source pages could not be fetched directly, so details come from search-result summaries of vendor and press reporting, and several CVE numbers and fix versions should be checked against the vendor advisories linked below before action. Watch this week for: **Microsoft Patch Tuesday on 13 October**, Fortinet releasing or confirming FortiMail fixed builds, public naming of the vendors in the Bitget compromise, more NetScaler exploitation reporting, and ASOS and Osaka Metropolitan University confirming scope.

## 1. Risk Watch

### 1.1 Fortinet FortiMail unauthenticated file write, CVE-2026-104286 (ONGOING)
Fortinet advisory **FG-IR-26-175** (1 Oct) describes a path traversal combined with improper NULL-byte handling, CVSS **9.8**. Crafted HTTP/HTTPS requests let an unauthenticated attacker write arbitrary files to the underlying system. Fortinet confirmed in-the-wild exploitation, and CISA added it to KEV with a federal deadline of 4 Oct. Affected: FortiMail **8.0.0–8.0.1, 7.6.0–7.6.6, 7.4.0–7.4.8** (one outlet also lists 7.2.x). The advisory lists fixes in **7.4.9, 7.6.7 and 8.0.2**; secondary reports say release notes for 7.6.7/7.4.9 now exist, but this is unconfirmed, and the status of 7.2 is conflicting. No actor or victim count has been disclosed. File-write bugs on mail gateways commonly lead to webshells and persistence.
**Action:** Confirm the current fixed build on the Fortinet PSIRT page and upgrade; until then restrict FortiMail admin/web interfaces to trusted networks. Hunt for unexpected files in web-served directories, new or modified scripts, and anomalous POSTs containing `%00` or `../` sequences.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/critical-fortimail-zero-day-flaw.html) · [Field Effect](https://fieldeffect.com/blog/patches-pending-actively-exploited-fortimail-flaw) · [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/exploited-fortinet-fortimail-zero-day-calls-for-urgent-action/amp) · [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/02/fortinet-sounds-the-alarm-over-actively-exploited-fortimail-zero-day/5300803)

### 1.2 Citrix NetScaler zero-days CVE-2026-88771 / CVE-2026-88772, plus CVE-2026-88779 (ONGOING)
Citrix bulletin **CTX697096** (27 Sep) fixes two unauthenticated RCE flaws, both CVSS **9.5** and both exploited before a patch existed. CVE-2026-88771 affects NetScaler ADC and Gateway in default configuration. CVE-2026-88772 affects deployments with DTLS enabled, which is the default on VPN vServers. The same advisory fixes six other issues, including **CVE-2026-88773** (HTTP request smuggling, 9.3). CISA added both RCE flaws to KEV on 27 Sep and told agencies to check for prior compromise before patching, since patching or rebooting can destroy evidence. On 4 Oct CISA added **CVE-2026-88779**, a SAML-related memory-buffer flaw that attackers are using to crash appliances (DoS). Shadowserver counts about **22,000** exposed instances. Start-of-exploitation dates conflict: 26 Sep per some outlets, and several weeks before the patch per CSA.
**Action:** Patch to the builds in CTX697096. Before patching, preserve memory and disk images and review for webshells, new admin accounts, unexpected `nsroot` sessions, and outbound connections from the appliance. Rotate credentials and secrets for anything the appliance could reach, such as LDAP bind accounts and VPN certificates.
Sources: [CISA alert](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/09/27/critical-zero-day-vulnerabilities-exploited-citrix-netscaler-adc-gateway) · [Help Net Security (CVE-2026-88779)](https://helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/05/cisa-flags-new-exploited-netscaler-flaw-as-attackers-crash-appliances-cve-2026-88779) · [BleepingComputer](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/cisa-orders-feds-to-patch-exploited-citrix-flaws-by-wednesday) · [Tenable FAQ](https://tenable.com/blog/frequently-asked-questions-about-reported-citrix-netscaler-zero-day-vulnerabilities) · [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/security/citrix-netscaler-rce-vulnerabilities-active-attack/)

### 1.3 Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager authentication bypass, CVE-2026-76504 (ONGOING)
CISA added this flaw to KEV (CVSS **9.8**, API session-based authentication) after Cisco PSIRT reported exploitation beginning in September. A remote, unauthenticated attacker can obtain administrative access. All deployments are affected regardless of configuration, and **there is no workaround**. Fixed releases: **26.2.1, 26.1.2.1, 20.18.4.1, 20.15.6.1, 20.12.8.2, 20.9.10.1**. CISA gave a three-day deadline. This follows the earlier exploited SD-WAN bugs CVE-2026-20127 and CVE-2026-20182, the latter attributed by Cisco to **UAT-8616**. No attribution has been reported for this CVE, so the link is only a pattern, not a finding.
**Action:** Upgrade to a fixed release. Do not expose the SD-WAN Manager to the internet. Review for unexpected admin accounts, API tokens, and configuration pushes to edge devices since September.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/cisa-adds-exploited-cisco-catalyst-sd.html) · [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/cisco-patches-exploited-catalyst-sd-wan-zero-day-vulnerability/amp/)

### 1.4 Bitget $387.5M theft via zero-days in third-party security products (DEVELOPING)
Bitget says attackers reached an internal management system by exploiting a zero-day in a third-party security product, then injected fraudulent withdrawal commands into wallet backend systems. SlowMist and Mandiant reportedly found two security appliances compromised with zero-days. CEO Gracy Chen says private keys and cold wallets were not compromised and attributes the attack to North Korean actors, based on IP behavior, on-chain analysis, and wallet overlaps with earlier DPRK thefts. The attacker made small test transfers first to probe risk controls. Frozen funds are small (**$0.63M–$1.1M**, sources differ). The protection fund will cover the loss. The vendors have not been named, and it is unconfirmed whether the appliances are among the products in the exploited-CVE lists above.
**Action:** Treat security appliances (VPN, mail, management plane) as tier-0. Review withdrawal-approval paths for out-of-band controls, and watch for vendor disclosures that match this intrusion.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/bitget-hacked-via-zero-day-in-third-party-security-products) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/bitget-confirms-third-party-zero-day.html) · [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-09-28-bitget-attacker-tested-risk-controls-small-transfers-388-million-theft-ceo-says-417045) · [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bitget-hack-zero-day-slowmist-investigation)

## 4. Threat Watch
- **UAT-11587 / Antino backdoor (China-nexus):** Cisco Talos (30 Sep) reports espionage against government and policy bodies in **eight Asian countries** since September 2025. Victim counts vary by source, from 10 confirmed up to 16 organizations. Phishing leads to a five-stage chain (HTA, Windows Script Host, .NET deserialization) and sideloading of the Rust backdoor Antino through a signed Microsoft diagnostic binary. Antino uses an Outlook mailbox for commands and OneDrive for file transfer via the Microsoft Graph API, so traffic looks like normal Microsoft 365 use. Detection works best on the endpoint: look for unusual processes calling `graph.microsoft.com`. [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-antino-backdoor-m365-c2-20261004-csa-style/) · [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/200264/apt/antino-backdoor-uses-your-inbox-as-its-control-panel.html) · [eSecurity Planet](https://www.esecurityplanet.com/cybersecurity-threats/news-uat-11587-antino-backdoor-microsoft-365-cloudflare/)
