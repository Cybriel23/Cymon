## 1. Risk Watch

### 1.1 Ivanti Connect Secure chain, CVE-2026-104935 (ONGOING, follow-up)
Volexity attributes the chain to **UTA0576**: an authentication bypass in welcome.cgi plus a file-write flaw in wu.cgi (tracked together as **CVE-2026-104935**, Ivanti CVSS **8.6**) give code execution, and a third flaw in how the dsinvoked broker trusts DSCache configuration data gives root, even on a patched appliance. Fixes for the first two are in **22.8R2.5, 25.1.1.2, 25.1.3.0 and 25.1.3.1**. The escalation flaw reportedly has **no CVE and no fix**, with Volexity saying a fix is weeks away. The actor deployed no malware or web shell, re-exploited the device for each command and tampered with logs, so Ivanti's Integrity Checker Tool does not detect it. Earliest activity is dated **1 Oct** (Defused) or **3 Oct** (Volexity). CISA has added the flaw to KEV. I found nothing newer than 13 to 14 Oct.
**Action:** Upgrade to a fixed build, remove internet exposure of portal and management interfaces where possible, treat pre-patch exposure as potential compromise (capture memory and network forensics, rotate credentials and certificates held on the device), and check Ivanti's advisory page daily for the escalation fix.
Sources: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/attacker-chains-three-ivanti-connect.html) · [BleepingComputer](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/ivanti-warns-of-connect-secure-zero-day-exploited-in-attacks) · [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/security/ivanti-zero-day-july-cisa-patch-no-fix/) · [iTnews](https://itnews.com.au/news/attackers-chain-three-ivanti-vpn-zero-days-for-root-access-629621)

### 1.2 French supermarket refrigeration attacks, Danfoss controllers (DEVELOPING, follow-up)
The overnight attack of **11 to 12 Oct** forced defrost cycles and pushed temperatures up, causing food loss and, per some reports, fires. Store counts vary by outlet (roughly 250 to more than 500); named chains include Intermarché, Coopérative U, Carrefour Market, Monoprix, Leclerc and Super U. Danfoss says most affected equipment is legacy and no longer receives security updates. A French government source said ANSSI had identified a vulnerability in software from a Danish company and contacted the vendor, while CERT-FR's alert did not tie the attack to a specific flaw. CISA warned in 2025 about Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA flaws (**CVE-2025-41450** authentication bypass, **CVE-2025-41451** command injection). The Paris prosecutor is investigating. No actor, claim, ransom demand or arrest has been reported, and the entry method is not publicly established.
**Action:** Find internet-reachable refrigeration and building-management controllers, put them behind VPN or allow-lists, isolate end-of-life units, replace default or shared credentials and review logs for unexpected defrost or setpoint changes.
Sources: [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/europe/french-supermarkets-refrigerator-shutdown-cyberattack) · [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/cyberattack-shuts-down-refrigerators-at-french-supermarkets/amp/) · [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/danfoss-fridge-attack-france-supermarket/)

### 1.3 IDCF Cloud (SoftBank), Japan (ONGOING, follow-up)
The **7 Oct** ransomware attack on East Japan Region 1 hit about **495** organisations. Management consoles were disabled in all regions pending security verification, and I found no restoration announcement (latest coverage up to 16 Oct). The provider initially said data could only be restored from customers' own backups; a single vendor blog dated 14 Oct says the **194** customers using the snapshot feature might get copies back, case by case, which is unconfirmed. The company reports no confirmed unauthorized access in other zones and regions. Entry route and actor are undisclosed; the attackers' deletion claims remain unverified.
**Action:** IDCF customers: keep rebuild-from-offline-backup plans and check IDC Frontier's notices directly. Others: confirm that backups and hypervisor credentials are not reachable from your cloud provider's management plane.
Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/ransomware-attack-disrupts-japans-idcf-cloud-used-by-govt-clients/) · [SDxCentral](https://www.sdxcentral.com/news/softbank-national-cloud-arm-attacked-in-seven-minutes-taking-japan-infrastructure-offline/) · [Nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/en/news/yjj2026100700867/) · [hard2bit](https://hard2bit.com/en/blog/idcf-cloud-ransomware-snapshots-backups-outside-cloud-provider/)

### 1.4 Windows Shell Mark-of-the-Web bypass, CVE-2026-96951 (ONGOING, follow-up)
The one actively exploited flaw in October Patch Tuesday (a very large release, about 1,400 fixes) is a Windows Shell security feature bypass that defeats Mark of the Web, so downloaded or emailed files skip SmartScreen-style warnings. A report says CISA ordered federal agencies to patch by Friday (16 Oct), which I could not confirm in the KEV entry. No actor or exploit chain has been named.
**Action:** Confirm the October cumulative update is deployed, prioritising endpoints that open email attachments and downloads; do not rely on Mark of the Web alone to gate downloaded files.
Sources: [SOC Prime](https://socprime.com/blog/cve-2026-96951-analysis/) · [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/security/microsoft-massive-security-update-1400-vulnerabilities/) · [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/microsoft/microsoft-october-2026-patch-tuesday-fixes-1-411-flaws-3-zero-days/)
