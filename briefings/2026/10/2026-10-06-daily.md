### 1.1 FortiMail CVE-2026-104286: patches are finally out — the workaround era ends today (follow-up, RESOLVED, EXPLOITED)

**This is the single most actionable change in today's briefing.** For four days this item has been carried as an actively exploited **CVSS 9.8** flaw with a KEV deadline and no vendor remedy in existence. That is over. **FG-IR-26-175 now publishes concrete fixed builds**, and the advisory's **Updated Date reads 5 October 2026**. Verified at the primary source with two independently worded queries this morning, because the previous four re-verifications returned "upgrade to upcoming" and the change needed confirming rather than assuming: **FortiMail 8.0.0–8.0.1 → upgrade to 8.0.2 or above**; **7.6.0–7.6.6 → 7.6.7 or above**; **7.4.0–7.4.8 → 7.4.9 or above**; **7.2.0–7.2.9 → migrate to branch 7.4 or above** (7.2 gets no in-branch fix, and 7.4 is now patched, so that migration path is real for the first time). The flaw is unchanged: **path traversal (CWE-22)** combined with **improper neutralisation of NULL bytes (CWE-158)**, letting an **unauthenticated** attacker **write arbitrary files** through crafted HTTP/HTTPS requests to the IBE endpoint; found internally by **Gwendal Guégniaud** of Fortinet Product Security; **exploited in the wild**; **KEV due date was 4 October** and expired unmet. **Action:** patch to **8.0.2 / 7.6.7 / 7.4.9** today, and on 7.2.x plan the branch migration now rather than treating the workaround as indefinite. Three things to get right in sequence. First, **hunt before you patch** — patching closes the hole and destroys nothing, but reimaging does, and the published indicators describe full device persistence that survives a patch: **`/data/etc/ld.so.preload`** referencing an unattributable **`/data/lib/liblog.so`**, replaced **`/data/bin/webconsole`** and **`/data/bin/mailservice`**, modified **`/bin/smit`** and **`/data/etc/httpd.conf`**, a dropped **`/data/migadmin.tar.gz`**, cron entries referencing **`/migadmin`**, and an **`archive234`** account; the log artefact of exploitation is **IBE decryption errors containing invalid Base64 data**; block and hunt **79.141.169[.]187** and **45.129.0[.]192**. Second, **do not drop the compensating controls on patch day** — keep IBE disabled and management restricted until you have confirmed the fixed build is running and triage is clean, since a patched-but-already-implanted appliance is the realistic bad outcome here. Third, **update your compliance record rather than replacing it**: the correct documentation is now "workaround applied from 1 October while no fix existed, patched to *X* on 6 October," which is a defensible timeline; silently overwriting the workaround note loses the part that covers the expired deadline. Any finding means exposure of **mail content, routing configuration and every credential the appliance stores or brokers** — rotate accordingly. **Sources:** [Fortinet FG-IR-26-175](https://www.fortiguard.com/psirt/FG-IR-26-175) · [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog) · [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/02/fortinet-fortimail-vulnerability-cve-2026-104286/) · [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/02/fortinet-sounds-the-alarm-over-actively-exploited-fortimail-zero-day/5300803)

### 1.2 NetScaler CVE-2026-88779 "PitScaler 2": KEV deadline is tomorrow, and the crashes may have been deliberate cover (follow-up, ONGOING, EXPLOITED)

**The federal deadline is tomorrow, 7 October**, with the **forensic-triage flag set** — CISA ordered agencies on Sunday to patch by Wednesday *and* conduct triage, so patching alone does not discharge the requirement. The substantive addition since yesterday is an interpretation from **watchTowr's Benjamin Harris**, who suggests the flaw may have been **used to purposefully crash machines in order to make exploitation of CVE-2026-88771 faster**. That reframes the item: if correct, the denial of service was **instrumental rather than terminal**, and an appliance that rebooted is a candidate victim of the September intrusion set rather than merely an unavailable box. Treat it as a hypothesis from a credible researcher, not established fact — Citrix's own position remains availability-only impact ("we have not identified an impact on the integrity of customer data"), which is its current analysis of the memory-corruption primitive and not a guarantee. Also new: **Australia's ACSC has issued its own warning alongside CISA's**, and Citrix credits **Bishop Fox** and **watchTowr** with identifying the issue. Technical detail unchanged: **memory overflow (CWE-119)**, **CVSS v4.0 8.7**, in SAML processing, crashing the **`nsaaad`** service, with exploitation attempts carrying **shell commands hidden in the SAML username field** to fetch and run a payload; Citrix notes that "if the condition is triggered repeatedly, the service may remain unavailable." **Exposure precondition:** SAML must be configured as service provider (`add authentication samlAction`) or identity provider (`add authentication samlIdPProfile`); appliances with no SAML configuration are not exposed by this CVE. **Affected:** 14.1 before **14.1-73.41**, 13.1 before **13.1-64.28**, 14.1 FIPS before **14.1-73.41 FIPS**, 13.1 FIPS/NDcPP before **13.1-37.282**. **Action:** patch to **14.1-73.41 / 13.1-64.28** or the FIPS equivalents before tomorrow; apply Citrix's **Global Deny Lists** as interim control only, never as remediation. Then triage on the assumption that the crash was cover: **preserve `nsaaad` crash dumps and core files before the next restart**, search authentication logs for **usernames containing shell syntax or command separators**, look for an unexpected **`/v`** file on the appliance, and hunt outbound connections to **213.209.159[.]55**. Keep crash evidence, attempted commands and confirmed execution separate in your notes. **A reboot does not clean a compromised appliance.** And re-run the **CVE-2026-88771/88772** triage if you have not, because the **`sec_monitor` superuser written into `/flash/nsconfig/ns.conf` survives upgrade**: diff `ns.conf` for unexpected superusers, check **`/var/netscaler/logon/LogonPoint/.local_journal`** and **`.ctxs.receiver`**, diff **`/etc/httpd.conf`** for CSS-like paths mapped to PHP, verify **`/bin/sh`** is not mode **6555**, hunt **64.94.85[.]67**, **45.141.21[.]130**, **31.56.197[.]72:9090** and **23.27.143[.]20:9000**, and rotate everything the appliance brokers per **CTX694799**. ### 1.3 Dell System Update CVE-2026-86360: CVSS 9.6 unauthenticated remote path traversal to root, in the tool that pushes your firmware (NEW)

**Dell DSA-2026-324** patches five flaws in **Dell System Update (DSU)**, the command-line utility enterprises use to deploy **BIOS, firmware, driver and application updates** across **PowerEdge** fleets on Linux and Windows. The headline issue is **CVE-2026-86360**, **CVSS 9.6**: a **path traversal** that an **unauthenticated attacker with remote access** can exploit for **filesystem access leading to arbitrary code execution with root privileges**. Read the target, not just the score — this is not an application server, it is the mechanism by which firmware reaches your hardware, so successful exploitation offers a path to persistence below the operating system and a trusted channel into every server the tool manages. The four others: **CVE-2026-86361** (**CVSS 8.2**, incorrect permission assignment, local privilege escalation), **CVE-2026-86362** (**CVSS 8.2**, improper access control, local privilege escalation), **CVE-2026-63697** (**CVSS 7.6**, improper certificate validation, remote code execution by a high-privileged attacker) and **CVE-2026-71168** (**CVSS 7.3**, path traversal, local). **Affected: all versions prior to 2.3.0.0. Fixed: 2.3.0.0 or later.** **No reported in-the-wild exploitation yet**, and no public PoC located this morning. **Action:** upgrade DSU to **2.3.0.0** across your management estate, and treat this as a management-plane change rather than an endpoint patch — inventory where DSU actually runs, which is usually more places than the server team expects, including build images, provisioning hosts and jump boxes. Two things worth doing alongside the upgrade. First, **check whether any DSU listener or repository endpoint is reachable from a general-purpose network segment**; an unauthenticated remote path traversal matters very differently on a host reachable from user VLANs than on one confined to an out-of-band management network, and this is the week to find out which you have. Second, note that **CVE-2026-63697 is an improper certificate validation flaw in an update-delivery tool**, which means the integrity of the update channel itself was weak — so if you have a requirement to verify firmware provenance, the honest position is that updates deployed through unpatched DSU had a weaker guarantee than assumed. Dell notes that state-sponsored groups have previously targeted other Dell vulnerabilities; the realistic risk window here is the period between public disclosure and your patch, because a 9.6 unauthenticated path traversal against a known enterprise tool is exactly the shape that gets reverse-engineered quickly. **Sources:** [Dell DSA-2026-324](https://www.dell.com/support/kbdoc/en-us/000515843/dsa-2026-324-security-update-for-dell-system-update-dsu-vulnerabilities) · [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/new-dell-system-update-flaw-lets-hackers-gain-root-privileges/)

### 1.4 Denmark's Central Population Register: 8.8 million of 11 million records enumerated through one company's legitimate access (NEW, ONGOING)

**Denmark's CPR register** — the national civil registry — was systematically harvested after threat actors **misused a private Danish company's authorised access**. The Danish Data Protection Agency's description of the method is the whole lesson: the attack "involved some form of brute-forcing to enumerate valid CPR numbers, and then extract the related data from each entry." No CVE, no exploit, no malware; a legitimate integration driven at machine speed until it had walked most of a country's population. **Scope: approximately 8.8 million records out of roughly 11 million**, or about **80% of the registry**, which covers residents, people who have emigrated and the deceased. **Exposed fields:** names, addresses, **CPR numbers** (the Danish national identifier), dates of birth, **birth registration details, marital status, family relationships, Church of Denmark affiliation, and legal incapacitation information**. Records belonging to individuals with **name and address protection were not exposed**. **Timeline:** the incident occurred in **September 2026**; the CPR administration **discovered it on Friday 2 October** and **immediately blocked the company's access**, determining scope over the weekend; the case was **reported to Datatilsynet on Sunday**, which says it is still processing the notification before assessing circumstances. **Police are investigating**; **no actor attribution**, and **the company has not been publicly named**. Minister **Christina Egelund** called it "a deeply serious incident" and briefed the Folketing's Business and Digitalisation Committee; a **cyber hotline** has been established and a security review of the CPR system launched. **Action:** nothing to patch, and the generalisable control work is where the value is. If you expose a lookup API to partners — customer verification, eligibility checks, address validation, KYC — ask three questions today. **Does any single partner credential have a rate limit that would stop enumeration of your entire record set, and do you know what that limit is numerically?** **Do you alert on a partner's query volume or query-pattern change, as opposed to only on authentication failures?** A valid credential making valid queries generates no security events in most architectures, which is precisely why this ran for a month. **And can you reconstruct, per partner, which records were accessed** — because Denmark's ministry could establish that 8.8 million entries were reached but "did not specify which additional data was accessed in each case," and that ambiguity is what makes notification and remediation so hard here. For organisations with Danish staff, customers or operations: the **CPR plus address plus family-relationship** combination supports identity fraud and highly credible social engineering that cites real relatives, so brief affected people on the specific shape of the follow-on risk rather than issuing a generic phishing warning. **Sources:** [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/denmark-population-registry-data-breach-affects-88-million-people/) · [The Copenhagen Post](https://cphpost.dk/2026-10-05/life-in-denmark/cpr-data-breach-exposes-personal-details-of-8-8-million-people-in-denmark/) · [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/200437/data-breach/denmark-s-population-registry-breached-8-8-million-affected.html) · [GBHackers](https://gbhackers.com/denmark-confirms-major-security-incident/)

### 1.5 Rejetto HFS CVE-2026-61500: now under active internet-wide scanning — with a scale correction and a better fix target (follow-up, EXPLOITED)

Scanning for the HFS session-forgery flaw is now being reported directly, and two corrections to yesterday's framing are warranted. **First, the scale is smaller than yesterday's briefing implied.** VulnCheck's **Caitlin Condon** describes the Canary Intelligence honeypot observations as "**small-scale reconnaissance from a single China Telecom IP address** probing deployments in Japan and the United States." Yesterday's edition reported VulnCheck telemetry via Patrick Garrity as exploitation by "an actor in China targeting real vulnerable hosts in the US" and listed two further IPs; the more precise characterisation now available is **limited probing from one source**, not a broad campaign. The risk assessment does not change — a **public PoC against a pre-auth flaw in an internet-exposed file server** will attract volume — but if you briefed this as mass exploitation yesterday, correct it, because credibility on scale is what buys you attention on the next one. **Second, patch further than 3.2.1:** the current stable release is **3.3.4**, and that is the better target. The mechanism is worth retaining as a design lesson: HFS **derives its session-cookie signing key from the non-cryptographic `Math.random()`** and **leaks raw `Math.random()` outputs to unauthenticated users** through the login response path; V8's `Math.random()` is **xorshift128+**, fully reversible, so an attacker collects outputs from a few failed logins, **recovers the PRNG state and therefore the signing key**, forges an administrator cookie, and executes code via the **`server_code`** configuration feature. **Affected 3.0.0–3.2.0; fixed 3.2.1; current stable 3.3.4.** The flaw was surfaced by **Anthropic's Mythos** model, which identified that **Z3 could recover the seed** from the leaked outputs, and the disclosure-to-probing gap was roughly **one day**. **Still not in KEV**, verified programmatically this morning. **Action:** upgrade to **3.3.4**, or take the service off the internet if you cannot. The detection signature is the useful part and is unchanged: because the attack forges a *valid* session, **your logs will show successful administrator logins with no preceding failures from that source** — which is also why failed-login alerting will not catch it. Hunt **unexpected `server_code` configuration changes** (the RCE mechanism) and review file-transfer logs for unauthorised access. Given the revised scale, "treat every exposed instance as compromised" is now stronger than the evidence supports; **treat exposed instances in the US and Japan as needing triage, and all exposed instances as needing the patch this week**. **Sources:** [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/rejetto-hfs-servers-now-actively-scanned-for-critical-rce-flaw/) · [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/exploitation-hits-rejetto-hfs-vulnerability-discovered-by-ai/) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/attackers-target-rejetto-hfs-flaw-that.html) · [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/200444/ai/anthropic-mythos-found-a-bug-in-rejetto-hfs-attackers-are-now-exploiting-it.html) · [PoC / Docker lab](https://github.com/aramosf/CVE-2026-61500)
