## Executive Summary

### 1.1 NetScaler CVE-2026-88779 "PitScaler 2": the reboots were exploitation, not a patch bug — patch is out, KEV deadline Wednesday (NEW, ONGOING, EXPLOITED — corrects Saturday's assessment)

On Friday, administrators running the September emergency build **14.1-73.37** reported appliances rebooting in loops, and Citrix described a "newly seen SAML issue" crashing the **`nsaaad`** service. Saturday's edition of this briefing assessed that as a patch regression with an availability impact and advised against rolling back. That assessment was wrong and should be discarded. Citrix released patches **early Sunday 4 October** for **CVE-2026-88779**, a **memory overflow** (**CWE-119**, **CVSS v4.0 8.7**) in SAML processing, stating plainly: "Citrix has observed targeted attacks on unmitigated NetScaler deployments which can lead to Denial of Service." Reporting describes authentication requests containing **shell commands hidden in the username field** intended to retrieve and execute malicious scripts, and **Kevin Beaumont** confirmed exploitation attempts against honeypots with malware binaries recovered; **watchTowr Labs** was also engaged on the NetScaler set. **CISA added it to KEV on 4 October with a due date of 7 October** and the **forensic-triage flag set** — the sixth exploited NetScaler flaw added in 2026. **Exposure precondition:** the appliance must have SAML configured as either service provider (`add authentication samlAction`) or identity provider (`add authentication samlIdPProfile`); appliances with no SAML configuration are not exposed by this CVE. **Affected:** 14.1 before **14.1-73.41**, 13.1 before **13.1-64.28**, 14.1 FIPS before **14.1-73.41 FIPS**, 13.1 FIPS/NDcPP before **13.1-37.282**. **Fixed:** those same builds or later. Note that Citrix characterises the impact as availability only — "we have not identified an impact on the integrity of customer data" — but that is Citrix's current analysis of the memory-corruption primitive, not a guarantee, and the observed payload-retrieval attempts argue for treating affected boxes as suspect rather than merely crashed. **Action:** patch to **14.1-73.41 / 13.1-64.28** (or the FIPS equivalents) today — this is a two-day KEV window, not a two-week one. Apply Citrix's **Global Deny Lists** as an interim control if a maintenance window genuinely cannot be found, but do not treat IP blocking as remediation. Then triage rather than assume the reboots were noise: **preserve `nsaaad` crash dumps and core files before the next restart**, search authentication logs for **usernames containing shell syntax or command separators**, look for an unexpected **`/v`** file on the appliance (observed malware download destination), and hunt outbound connections to **213.209.159[.]55**. Keep crash evidence, attempted commands and confirmed execution separate in your notes — a crash alone does not prove successful execution, but a crafted username immediately preceding one is a strong signal. **A reboot does not clean a compromised appliance.** And separately, the September intrusion set is still unresolved for many: the **`sec_monitor` superuser written into `/flash/nsconfig/ns.conf` survives upgrade**, so re-run the **CVE-2026-88771/88772** triage — diff `ns.conf` for unexpected superusers, check **`/var/netscaler/logon/LogonPoint/.local_journal`** and **`.ctxs.receiver`**, diff **`/etc/httpd.conf`** for CSS-like paths mapped to PHP, verify **`/bin/sh`** is not mode **6555**, hunt **64.94.85[.]67**, **45.141.21[.]130**, **31.56.197[.]72:9090** and **23.27.143[.]20:9000**, and rotate everything the appliance brokers per **CTX694799**. Beaumont's firmware scanning during the first PitScaler wave found **fewer than 10% of boxes patched** while mass exploitation was underway; assume the same curve here. **Sources:** [Citrix CTX697174](https://support.citrix.com/support-home/kbsearch/article?articleNumber=CTX697174) · [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/citrix-patches-netscaler-saml-zero-day-exploited-in-attacks/) · [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/exploitation-of-citrix-netscaler-zero-day-hits-appliances-patched-days-earlier/) · [Cyber Security News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/citrix-netscaler-saml-0-day-vulnerability/) · [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

### 1.2 Rejetto HFS CVE-2026-61500: CVSS 9.8 unauthenticated admin-session forgery, public PoC, exploited within a day (NEW, EXPLOITED)

**Rejetto HTTP File Server** — a small, widely deployed file-sharing server that tends to live on exactly the hosts nobody inventories — carries an authentication bypass chaining to remote code execution, and it is already being exploited. The root cause is a textbook cryptographic failure worth reading as a design lesson: HFS **derives its session-cookie signing key from `Math.random()`**, and separately **leaks raw `Math.random()` outputs to unauthenticated users through the login response path**. V8's `Math.random()` uses **xorshift128+**, which is fully reversible, so an attacker collects outputs from a handful of failed login attempts, **recovers the PRNG state and therefore the signing key**, forges an administrator session cookie, and executes arbitrary code through the **`server_code`** configuration feature. **CVSS 9.8**, **CWE-338**, **no authentication, no user interaction, low attack complexity**. **Affected: 3.0.0 through 3.2.0. Fixed: 3.2.1.** A **working PoC and Docker lab are public on GitHub**. Exploitation is confirmed: **VulnCheck's Patrick Garrity** reported canary detections of "an actor in China targeting real vulnerable hosts in the US," with initial activity Thursday night against targets in the **US and Japan**, followed by four further attempts from **173.239.211.248** and **173.239.211.249** — two addresses in one US subnet, assessed as proxy infrastructure rather than the true origin. The flaw was surfaced by **Anthropic's Mythos** model, which identified that **Z3 could recover the PRNG seed** from the leaked outputs; the gap from disclosure to in-the-wild exploitation was about **one day**. **Action:** upgrade to **3.2.1** immediately, and if you cannot, take the service off the internet — with a public PoC against a 9.8 pre-auth bug in a small file server, "we'll patch next sprint" is not a position. Because the attack forges a *valid* session rather than brute-forcing one, **your authentication logs will show successful admin logins with no preceding failures from that source** — that is the detection signature, and it is also why failed-login alerting will not catch this. Hunt for **unexpected `server_code` configuration changes** (the RCE mechanism), review file-transfer logs for unauthorised access, and **treat any HFS instance that was internet-exposed since Thursday as potentially compromised** rather than merely vulnerable. The broader point for your vulnerability-management process: AI-assisted discovery is compressing the disclosure-to-exploitation window, and a one-day gap leaves no room for a monthly patch cycle on internet-facing software. **Sources:** [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/03/anthropics-super-bug-hunting-model-mythos-is-hardcore-good-at-math-as-latest-vuln-under-attack-shows/5300933) · [Strix CVE detail](https://www.strix.ai/cve/CVE-2026-61500) · [PoC / Docker lab](https://github.com/aramosf/CVE-2026-61500) · [WindowsForum analysis](https://windowsforum.com/news/cve-2026-61500-attackers-exploit-rejetto-hfs-session-forgery-rce-upgrade-to-3-2-1.447112/)

### 1.3 FortiMail CVE-2026-104286: KEV deadline expired yesterday, still no patch (follow-up, ONGOING, EXPLOITED)

Re-verified at the primary source this morning, four days after the KEV listing: **Fortinet's FG-IR-26-175 still publishes no fixed build.** Every solution field reads "upgrade to **upcoming**" — **8.0.2 or above**, **7.6.7 or above**, **7.4.9 or above** — and **7.2.x** is told to migrate to the **7.4 branch**, which is itself unpatched. **The CISA due date was yesterday, 4 October, and has passed with no vendor remedy available.** The flaw is unchanged: **CVSS 9.8**, **path traversal (CWE-22)** combined with **improper neutralisation of NULL bytes (CWE-158)**, letting an **unauthenticated** attacker **write arbitrary files** via crafted HTTP/HTTPS requests to the IBE endpoint; found internally by **Gwendal Guégniaud** of Fortinet Product Security; **exploited in the wild**. **Affected: 8.0.0–8.0.1, 7.6.0–7.6.6, 7.4.0–7.4.8, 7.2.0–7.2.9** — every supported version. Neither Fortinet nor CISA has published exploitation scale, victim count, first-exploitation date or attribution. **Action:** the workaround is your remediation and your compliance record. Apply all three that fit: **disable IBE** (GUI: Encryption → IBE → IBE Service "off", or the equivalent CLI `config system encryption ibe` / `set status disable`), **restrict webmail and management access to trusted private networks**, and **configure your WAF to block POST requests to `/ibe` containing `../`** — the cheapest compensating control if business need prevents dropping IBE. Then hunt, because the published indicators describe full device persistence: **`/data/etc/ld.so.preload`** referencing an unattributable **`/data/lib/liblog.so`**, replaced **`/data/bin/webconsole`** and **`/data/bin/mailservice`**, modified **`/bin/smit`** and **`/data/etc/httpd.conf`**, a dropped **`/data/migadmin.tar.gz`**, cron entries referencing **`/migadmin`**, and an **`archive234`** account. The log artefact of exploitation is **IBE decryption errors containing invalid Base64 data**. Block and hunt **79.141.169[.]187** and **45.129.0[.]192**. **Capture device state before you reboot or reimage** — an `ld.so.preload` implant vanishes with a clean reinstall done before evidence collection. Any finding means exposure of **mail content, routing configuration and every credential the appliance stores or brokers**; rotate accordingly. Document the workaround as your **BOD 26-04** mitigation — that is what CISA's required-action language contemplates when no vendor update exists, and with the deadline now expired, having that documentation dated before yesterday matters. **Sources:** [Fortinet FG-IR-26-175](https://www.fortiguard.com/psirt/FG-IR-26-175) · [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog) · [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/02/fortinet-sounds-the-alarm-over-actively-exploited-fortimail-zero-day/5300803) · [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/exploited-fortinet-fortimail-zero-day-calls-for-urgent-action/)

### 1.4 Zammad CVE-2026-102489 / CVE-2026-102490: deadline today, and a correction to the vendor's position (follow-up, EXPLOITED)

**Both CVEs remain in KEV with a due date of today, 5 October.** A correction to earlier editions of this briefing, which reported Zammad's position that it had not received technical details for the root-escalation half: the vendor's community thread was updated on **1 October at 19:48**, with Zammad developer **fliebe92** stating that "we have now received the details of CVE-2026-102490 from DIVD, and we are working on it." Zammad also published an assessment that narrows the risk materially: **"This issue cannot be exploited remotely on its own. An attacker would already need access to your server."** That is consistent with CISA's own naming — **CVE-2026-102489** is **session fixation** leading to **remote code execution as the `zammad` user**, **CVE-2026-102490** is **improper privilege management** allowing that local user to **escalate to root** — and it means the LPE is the second link in a chain, not an independent remote risk. **There is still no fix for CVE-2026-102490.** Version data: **CVE-2026-102489 affects 6.3.0–6.5.4** and is present-but-not-exploitable in **7.0.0–7.1.3** for environmental reasons; **CVE-2026-102490 spans v1.5.0 through the v7.1.0 alpha**. Exploitation context is the **21 September** intrusion at **DIVD**, where an agentic attacker ran **password spraying and a man-in-the-middle attack simultaneously** and completed the chain "in seconds," pivoting to other services and exfiltrating data, limited by **network segmentation**. **Action:** upgrade to **7.2.0** today — that closes the remote entry point, which is the link that matters, and is the compliant action available. Until the LPE is fixed, operate the host on the assumption that **any code execution on it equals root**: isolate it, strip stored credentials and API tokens for integrated systems off it, and keep it outside shared trust boundaries. Get any internet-facing instance behind authentication. Preserve application and authentication logs **before** remediating, and run **DIVD's `cve-2026-102489_ioc_check_script_v2.sh`**. Because a helpdesk holds ticket attachments, customer correspondence and whatever credentials users paste into tickets, scope any finding as a **data-exposure incident**, not merely a server compromise. **Sources:** [Zammad community advisory](https://community.zammad.org/t/take-care-local-privilege-escalation-cve-2026-102490-is-reported-as-being-actively-exploited/21297) · [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog) · [DIVD-2026-00015](https://csirt.divd.nl/cases/DIVD-2026-00015/) · [Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/zerodays-dutch-institute/)

### 1.5 Six South Korean financial institutions breached with an open-source AI penetration-testing framework (NEW, ONGOING)

**President Lee Jae Myung has ordered a comprehensive investigation** after data breaches spread across South Korea's financial sector, and the forensic detail is the reason this belongs in Risk Watch rather than Incident Watch. Investigators recovered a **Chinese-language string translating as "AI autonomous penetration testing console,"** linked to **ARTEX AI**, an **open-source penetration-testing framework** that automates reconnaissance, vulnerability discovery and attack planning. Reporting also describes **credential stuffing** at automated scale using previously compromised credentials. Affected institutions: **Shinhan Bank** (~**25,000** customers), **KB Kookmin Bank**, **Hana Bank**, **BNK Busan Bank**, **Yegaram Savings Bank** (~**40,000**), and **Hyundai Capital** (**146** housing-loan agents). Exposed fields include names, telephone numbers, **annual income, loan limits** and **resident registration numbers** — the Korean national identifier, which makes this a durable identity-fraud exposure rather than a password-reset event. The **Financial Services Commission** and **Financial Supervisory Service** have held emergency meetings, the **National Police Agency** has opened a preliminary investigation, and regulators are calling for "AI attacks responding with AI," specifically **AI-driven security testing** and **phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication**. **Action:** the transferable lesson is that commodity agentic tooling has lowered the cost of chaining reconnaissance, credential stuffing and exploitation against a whole sector at once, so controls that assumed attacker labour was the limiting factor need revisiting. Concretely: confirm that **credential stuffing against your customer-facing authentication is detected and rate-limited at the identity layer, not just the WAF**; check whether you alert on **high-volume authentication success following high-volume failure** from distributed sources, which is the signature of a successful stuffing run; move privileged and customer-facing authentication to **phishing-resistant factors**; and test whether your bulk-data-access alerting fires on **automated enumeration patterns** rather than only on volume thresholds. Read alongside Microsoft's finding, reported last week, that **AI has cut post-compromise attack time to minutes** — the two observations are the same trend measured from different ends. **Sources:** [GBHackers](https://gbhackers.com/south-korea-orders-investigation/) · [Cyber Security News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/south-korean-president-orders-full-security-checks/) · [DataBreaches.net](https://databreaches.net/2026/10/04/south-koreas-president-lee-jae-myung-orders-thorough-probe-into-data-breaches-at-local-banks/) · [Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/opinion/ai-hacking-spree-exposes-cracks-in-financial-sector-security/12904304)
