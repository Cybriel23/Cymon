## 1. Risk Watch

### 1.1 Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager CVE-2026-76504: unauthenticated takeover of your network controller, KEV deadline Saturday (NEW, EXPLOITED)

**CVE-2026-76504** is a **CVSS 9.8** authentication bypass in **Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager** (formerly vManage). The mechanism is mundane and therefore reliable: the API session-handling layer **fails to normalise URI encoding**, so a crafted HTTP request with encoded variations of **`j_security_check`** bypasses an authentication rule that was only ever meant to restrict one endpoint. **No credentials required.** Cisco's PSIRT says it became aware of exploitation **in September 2026 during review of a TAC support case** — the detail that matters, because it means compromise was discovered by victims rather than by telemetry, and Cisco has published **no victim count, no first-exploitation date, no actor and no objective**. Understand what this device is before triaging it: SD-WAN Manager holds the control plane for your WAN fabric, device certificates, templates and credentials, so a pre-auth bypass here is equivalent to administrative control of the network edge, not just one appliance. **Fixed releases: 20.9.10.1, 20.12.8.2, 20.15.6.1, 20.18.4.1, 26.1.2.1 and 26.2.1**; pre-20.9 trains must migrate. Cisco Managed Cloud is already on **20.15.605**. **CISA added it to KEV on 30 September with a due date of 3 October.** **Action:** take the Manager off the internet first — it should never have been reachable, and restricting access to trusted management hosts behind a firewall is faster than an upgrade window; isolate **TCP 443, 22 and 830** from direct exposure. Then upgrade. Before you upgrade, **hunt**: grep **`/var/log/nms/containers/service-proxy/serviceproxy-access.log`** and **`/var/log/nms/vmanage-server.log`** for **URI-encoded variants of `j_security_check`**, and treat any log entry referencing **`viptela-reserved-*`** system accounts as suspicious. Run **`request admin-tech`** to capture state before patching, and open a **Severity 3 TAC case** citing the CVE if you find anything — Cisco is clearly collecting on this. **Sources:** [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/cisco-warns-of-attackers-exploiting.html) · [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

### 1.2 NetScaler: the implant creates its own superuser and hides a shell behind fake CSS URLs — patching alone does not evict it (ONGOING, follow-up)

The vulnerabilities are unchanged — **CVE-2026-88771** (**CVSS 9.5**, pre-auth command injection, default configuration) and **CVE-2026-88772** (**CVSS 9.5**, DTLS memory overflow) — but **LevelBlue's post-exploitation analysis, published 1 October**, supplies the persistence detail that changes remediation. Exploitation is identifiable in authentication data containing variations of the strings **`pitboss`** and **`NSPPE`**. The dropped Perl script **`update_c08937.pl`** does four things: it **creates a local account named `sec_monitor` with superuser privileges by modifying `/flash/nsconfig/ns.conf`**; it archives **`/flash/nsconfig`** to **`/tmp/update_result_3567cs.tgz`**, uploads it to **64.94.85[.]67:443** and deletes the archive; it writes a PHP shell to **`/var/netscaler/logon/LogonPoint/.local_journal`** and edits **`/etc/httpd.conf`** to map it to **URLs that resemble legitimate NetScaler CSS resources**; and it sets **`/bin/sh` to mode 6555** (setuid/setgid). A separate **`main.py`** opens a reverse shell to **45.141.21[.]130:443** and kills SNMP processes. Read the first item again: **a configuration-resident superuser account survives an appliance upgrade**, because it lives in `ns.conf`, and a CSS-looking URL survives most web-log review. Mandiant separately confirms **suspected state-sponsored** actors, exploitation **since early September**, **dozens** of impacted organisations (Beaumont counted **100+** victims), and the **WHIPSHOT** / **SLAPSHOT** malware pair; Help Net Security adds the 88772 mechanism — **malformed or fragmented record headers** crash the **NSPPE** and corrupt the heap to reach root shellcode on FreeBSD. Exposure estimates now span three numbers and should be treated as estimates: **Shadowserver ~22,000**, **Censys ~42,000**, **Palo Alto Networks ~50,000** as of 27 September. **Action:** if you patched and did nothing else, you are probably still compromised. Today: **diff `/flash/nsconfig/ns.conf` for unexpected local accounts, `sec_monitor` first, and for any account with superuser binding you cannot attribute**; check **`/var/netscaler/logon/LogonPoint/.local_journal`** and the previously reported **`.ctxs.receiver`**; diff **`/etc/httpd.conf`** for handlers mapping non-PHP extensions or CSS-like paths to PHP; verify **`/bin/sh`** permissions are not **6555**; and look for **`/tmp/update_result_3567cs.tgz`**. Block and hunt **64.94.85[.]67**, **45.141.21[.]130**, **31.56.197[.]72:9090**, **23.27.143[.]20:9000**, plus the earlier **149.104.78.141**, **138.199.200.90** and DNS to **`instances.httpworkbench.com`**. Patch to **14.1-73.37** / **13.1-64.23** (FIPS **14.1-73.37 FIPS** / **13.1-37.279**), note **13.1 hit End of Maintenance on 15 September**, and follow **CTX694799** — terminate ICA/PCoIP sessions, rotate every credential the appliance holds or brokers, revoke and reissue certificates. **The KEV deadline was 30 September and has passed.** **Sources:** [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/citrix-netscaler-post-exploitation.html) · [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/30/cve-2026-88772-netscaler-exploitation-zero-day/) · [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/government-finance-orgs-targeted-in-weeks-long-netscaler-zero-day-attacks/) · [Cybersecurity Dive](https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/citrix-netscaler-exploitation-days-before-notification/831634/) · [Citrix CTX697096](https://support.citrix.com/external/article/CTX697096/citrix-netscaler-adc-and-citrix-netscale.html)

### 1.3 Apple CVE-2026-86950: PoC published, WhatsApp delivery claim retracted, KEV deadline tomorrow (follow-up, ESCALATED)

A **proof-of-concept for CVE-2026-86950 was published on 30 September** by researchers at **Calif** (Dion Blazakis, Josh Maine, Anna Groza), derived from **binary diffing iOS 26.7 against 26.7.1**, with generation scripts and a sample PDF in a public GitHub repository. The root cause is now public and specific: **inconsistent handling of out-of-range glyph coordinates during floating-point to fixed-point conversion** produces a bounding box too narrow for the glyph, so CoreGraphics allocates an undersized buffer and writes past it. What the PoC demonstrates is a **controlled out-of-bounds write — not code execution**, and that distinction should govern how you brief leadership: the hard part of the chain is still unpublished, but the primitive is now in everyone's hands, and the gap between primitive and exploit for a memory-corruption bug in a universally reachable parser is measured in weeks. On delivery: Calif noted **new PDF font-scanning code in WhatsApp 26.37.73 and 26.38.74** and initially claimed a WhatsApp delivery path, then **removed that claim within 85 minutes**; the published analysis now only asks whether the flaw was chained with WhatsApp bugs. Carry it as an open question, not a finding. Apple's own scoping is unchanged — exploited "in an extremely sophisticated attack against specific targeted individuals **on versions of iOS before iOS 27**," reported by **Meta Product Security**. **Action:** the deadline is tomorrow and the PoC is public, which together should end any remaining change-board debate. Push **iOS/iPadOS 26.7.1**, **macOS Tahoe 26.7.1** and **macOS Sequoia 15.8.1**, front-loading pre-iOS 27 devices and the usual target cohorts — executives, legal, M&A, communications, anyone working on high-risk jurisdictions. Because the vector is **file processing**, **Lockdown Mode** remains the highest-value control for named high-risk individuals, and it disables exactly the auto-render paths in play. There are still **no IOCs, no victim count and no attribution**. **Federal civilian agencies: due 2 October.** **Sources:** [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/apple-coregraphics-poc-emerges-as.html) · [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

### 1.4 Zimbra CVE-2026-73570: the full post-exploitation chain is now documented, and the patch has been out since July (NEW detail, EXPLOITED)

**CVE-2026-73570** (**CVSS 8.9**) is an **unauthenticated OS command injection** in Zimbra Collaboration Suite, reachable on servers where the optional **`zimbra-snmp`** package is installed and **SNMP notifications are enabled**, triggered by **specially crafted SMTP requests**. None of that is new — Zimbra fixed it in **10.1.20** in July, it was disclosed on **13 August**, and **CISA's KEV deadline was 24 August**. What published yesterday is the post-compromise chain, and it is thorough enough to be worth treating as an incident-response checklist rather than a vulnerability note: **JSP web shells deployed across Jetty and mailboxd paths**; **privilege escalation by modifying `/etc/pam.d/sudo`**; **extraction of Zimbra service secrets via `zmlocalconfig`**; **lateral movement using SSH identity keys and `rsync`**; and **database exfiltration of mailbox tables, credentials and mobile-device records**. Custom tooling: **Zimdown2**, a Go downloader; **Zimclient2**, a remote-access agent speaking WebSocket, TLS and raw TCP; and a Go credential extractor targeting **MySQL and LDAP**. Scanning of the injection path ran **28 July – 7 August**, i.e. before public disclosure. Attribution is **unknown**, targeting spans multiple industries and regions. **Action:** if you run Zimbra, the patch question is likely settled, so this is a hunting task. Verify **10.1.20 or later**; if you cannot patch, **uninstall `zimbra-snmp`** and **disable SNMP notifications**, and restrict SNMP and SMTP to trusted hosts. Then hunt: **diff `/etc/pam.d/sudo`**, inventory **JSP files under Jetty and mailboxd webapp directories** against a known-good baseline, review **`zmlocalconfig`** access and **`rsync`**/SSH activity from the mail host, and check `authorized_keys` on hosts the Zimbra service account can reach. Because service secrets and mailbox credentials were a documented objective, **rotate Zimbra LDAP and MySQL credentials and service secrets** rather than assuming the web shell was the whole of it. **Sources:** [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/attackers-exploit-zimbra-flaw-to-deploy.html) · [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

### 1.5 OpenInfra Europe's Artifactory was admin-compromised for 19 days — purge anything you pulled from nordix.org (NEW, SUPPLY CHAIN)

OpenInfra Europe disclosed on **30 September** that its self-hosted **JFrog Artifactory** instance at **`artifactory.nordix.org`** was compromised via **CVE-2026-82329**, an **unauthenticated authentication bypass** in Artifactory. The timeline is the lesson: the CVE was **disclosed 28 August**, **in-the-wild exploitation began 31 August** — the same day OpenInfra's instance fell — it was **added to KEV on 2 September**, and the breach was **discovered on 15 September only because a legitimate user was denied access**. Attackers **obtained admin privileges**, which put artifact contents, stored credentials and repository integrations all within reach. OpenInfra's own position is that **every artifact downloaded or installed from the repository between 28 August and 15 September is potentially compromised**, and that the **full scope and impact remain undetermined**. The instance was isolated on discovery. **Action:** this is a short, concrete task with a hard deadline nobody will chase you on, so do it now. Search your build caches, container images, CI configurations and dependency manifests for **`artifactory.nordix.org`**; for anything pulled in that window, **stop using it, remove it from pipelines and rebuild from an upstream source**, per OpenInfra's own guidance. Treat it as an identity incident as well as an artifact incident: **rotate any credential that was configured in, or passed through, that Artifactory integration**, and review CI audit logs across the 28 August – 15 September window for unexpected publishes. More broadly, the generalisable control is an inventory of which third-party artifact registries your builds actually reach — most organisations cannot answer that in under a day, which is why this class of notice goes unactioned. **Sources:** [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/30/openinfra-jfrog-artifactory-instance-compromised/) · [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)
