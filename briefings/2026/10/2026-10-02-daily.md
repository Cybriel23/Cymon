## 1. Risk Watch

### 1.1 Fortinet FortiMail CVE-2026-104286: unauthenticated file write on your mail gateway, KEV deadline Saturday (NEW, EXPLOITED)

**CVE-2026-104286** is a **CVSS 9.8** flaw in **FortiMail** that combines **path traversal (CWE-22)** with **improper neutralisation of NULL bytes (CWE-158)**, letting an **unauthenticated** attacker **write arbitrary files to the underlying system via crafted HTTP/HTTPS requests**. Fortinet published **FG-IR-26-175** on **1 October** and states exploitation is confirmed in the wild; the flaw was found internally by **Gwendal Guégniaud** of Fortinet Product Security, which means the vendor was hunting its own code while attackers were already using it. Understand what the file-write buys an attacker on this appliance: the published indicators describe a **shared-library implant** (**`/data/lib/liblog.so`**) loaded via **`/data/etc/ld.so.preload`**, replaced or added binaries **`/data/bin/webconsole`** and **`/data/bin/mailservice`**, modification of **`/bin/smit`** and **`/data/etc/httpd.conf`**, a dropped **`/data/migadmin.tar.gz`**, **cron entries referencing `/migadmin`**, and an **`archive234`** account — i.e. a full persistence chain on the device that reads, routes and archives your organisation's email. **Affected: 8.0.0–8.0.1, 7.6.0–7.6.6, 7.4.0–7.4.8, 7.2.0–7.2.9.** **Fixed: 8.0.2+, 7.6.7+, 7.4.9+; the 7.2 train gets no fix — migrate to 7.4 or above.** **CISA added it to KEV on 1 October with a due date of 4 October.** **Action:** patch today, but hunt first and do not reboot before you capture state, because an `ld.so.preload` implant is trivially lost to a clean reinstall you perform before collecting evidence. Check for the presence of **`/data/etc/ld.so.preload`** and any **`liblog.so`** you cannot attribute, diff **`/data/etc/httpd.conf`** and **`/bin/smit`** against a known-good build, enumerate local accounts for **`archive234`** or any other unexpected entry, review cron for **`/migadmin`** references, and look for **IBE decryption errors with invalid Base64 data** in logs — that is the exploitation artefact rather than the implant. Block and hunt **79.141.169[.]187** and **45.129.0[.]192**. If you cannot patch within the window, Fortinet's workarounds are to **disable IBE support via the CLI** and **restrict the management interface to trusted private networks** — and the second of those should be permanent regardless, since a mail gateway's admin interface has no business being internet-reachable. Treat any compromised appliance as having exposed **mail content, routing configuration and every credential the appliance stores or brokers**, and rotate accordingly. **Sources:** [Fortinet FG-IR-26-175](https://www.fortiguard.com/psirt/FG-IR-26-175) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/critical-fortimail-zero-day-flaw.html) · [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/fortinet-warns-of-critical-fortimail-flaw-exploited-in-zero-day-attacks/) · [Cyber Security News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/fortimail-0-day-vulnerability-exploited/) · [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

### 1.2 Zammad CVE-2026-102489 / CVE-2026-102490: the DIVD breach vulnerabilities are public, and the root-escalation half is unfixed in every version (NEW, EXPLOITED, follow-up resolved)

The tracked DIVD intrusion now has a named cause, and it generalises badly. **CVE-2026-102489** (**CVSS 9.4**) is an **unauthenticated session-hijack-to-remote-code-execution** flaw in **Zammad**, the open-source helpdesk; **CVE-2026-102490** (**CVSS 9.4**) is a **local privilege escalation from the `zammad` service user to root**. Chained, DIVD says they allowed an attacker to "hijack sessions, run code remotely and escalate privileges from the Zammad user to root, **in seconds**." Read the version matrix carefully, because the two CVEs do not line up: **CVE-2026-102489 affects 6.3.0 through 6.5.4** and is **present but not exploitable in 7.0.0–7.1.3 due to environmental conditions**, while **CVE-2026-102490 affects every release from v1.5.0 through the v7.1.0 alpha with no fix available**. So DIVD's guidance — **"upgrade to Zammad version 7, or take it offline"** — is a statement about removing the *pre-auth entry point*, not about being patched: anyone who achieves code execution as the service user by any other route still has a free path to root. DIVD's own framing of the attacker is unchanged and still the most useful detail for detection: the agent "**decided the next step itself, at the speed of light and sloppy logic**," conducting **password spraying while simultaneously running a man-in-the-middle attack** — noisy, self-defeating behaviour that a conventional operator would not produce. The intrusion occurred **21 September**, DIVD blocked access and opened **DIVD-2026-00014**, the Zammad work is tracked as **DIVD-2026-00015**, the vendor was notified **24 September**, **public scanning and victim notification began 26 September**, and details went public **30 September – 1 October**. Attackers **pivoted from Zammad to other services and exfiltrated data**; **network segmentation limited the damage** and DIVD is operating on an **assume-breach** basis. **Action:** inventory Zammad — it is the kind of system that gets installed by a support team and inherited by nobody, so check for instances outside your CMDB. Get any internet-facing instance behind authentication or offline today, then **upgrade to 7.x** to kill the pre-auth chain, and until **CVE-2026-102490** is fixed, treat the Zammad host as a system where any code execution equals root: isolate it, remove stored credentials and API tokens for integrated systems from it where you can, and do not let it share a trust boundary with anything else. Run **DIVD's `cve-2026-102489_ioc_check_script_v2.sh`** against your logs. Because a helpdesk holds ticket attachments, customer correspondence and often credentials pasted by users, **a compromise here is a data-exposure incident as much as a server incident** — scope it that way. **Sources:** [DIVD-2026-00015](https://csirt.divd.nl/cases/DIVD-2026-00015/) · [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/zammad-zero-days-exploited-in-ai-powered-divd-hack/) · [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/01/divd-agentic-ai-attack-breach/) · [DIVD CSIRT](https://csirt.divd.nl/2026/09/24/when-not-if/)

### 1.3 Apple CVE-2026-86950: KEV deadline is today, PoC public, no exploit chain yet (follow-up)

Nothing material changed in 24 hours, which is itself the status: **the PoC published on 30 September by Calif remains an out-of-bounds write primitive, not demonstrated code execution**, and no working chain has surfaced. The root cause is public — **inconsistent handling of out-of-range glyph coordinates during floating-point to fixed-point conversion** produces a bounding box too narrow for the glyph, so **CoreGraphics** allocates an undersized buffer and writes past it. The authors' initial claim of a **WhatsApp** delivery path was **retracted within 85 minutes** and remains an open question, not a finding, though their observation of new **PDF font-scanning code in WhatsApp 26.37.73 and 26.38.74** stands. Apple's scoping is unchanged: exploited "in an extremely sophisticated attack against specific targeted individuals **on versions of iOS before iOS 27**," reported by **Meta Product Security**. There are still **no IOCs, no victim count and no attribution**. **Action:** **the federal deadline is today, 2 October.** Push **iOS/iPadOS 26.7.1**, **macOS Tahoe 26.7.1** and **macOS Sequoia 15.8.1**, front-loading pre-iOS 27 devices and the high-risk cohorts — executives, legal, M&A, communications, anyone working on sensitive jurisdictions. Because the vector is **file processing**, **Lockdown Mode** remains the highest-value control for named high-risk individuals; it disables the auto-render paths in play. If you have not closed this by end of day, the honest position to record is that a public memory-corruption primitive in a universally reachable parser typically becomes a chain in weeks, not months. **Sources:** [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/apple-coregraphics-poc-emerges-as.html) · [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/200175/hacking/public-poc-released-for-apple-coregraphics-zero-day-cve-2026-86950.html) · [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

### 1.4 Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager CVE-2026-76504: KEV deadline tomorrow (follow-up, EXPLOITED)

Unchanged on the facts, live on the clock. **CVE-2026-76504** is a **CVSS 9.8** authentication bypass in **Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager** (formerly vManage): the API session layer **fails to normalise URI encoding**, so encoded variants of **`j_security_check`** bypass an authentication rule, **unauthenticated**, yielding administrative access to the controller for your WAN fabric, device certificates, templates and credentials. Cisco's PSIRT learned of exploitation **during review of a TAC support case in September 2026** — meaning victims found it, not telemetry — and has published **no victim count, no first-exploitation date, no actor and no objective**. **Fixed releases: 20.9.10.1, 20.12.8.2, 20.15.6.1, 20.18.4.1, 26.1.2.1 and 26.2.1**; pre-20.9 trains must migrate; Cisco Managed Cloud is on **20.15.605**. **CISA due date: 3 October — tomorrow.** **Action:** if the upgrade window is not available today, **remove the Manager from internet reachability now** and restrict **TCP 443, 22 and 830** to trusted management hosts — that is faster than patching and should be the permanent state. Before upgrading, capture state with **`request admin-tech`**, then hunt **`/var/log/nms/containers/service-proxy/serviceproxy-access.log`** and **`/var/log/nms/vmanage-server.log`** for **URI-encoded variants of `j_security_check`**, and treat any reference to **`viptela-reserved-*`** system accounts as suspicious. Open a **Severity 3 TAC case** citing the CVE if you find anything. **Sources:** [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/cisco-patches-exploited-catalyst-sd-wan-zero-day-vulnerability/) · [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/01/new-cisco-sd-wan-zero-day-exploited-in-the-wild-cve-2026-76504/) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/cisa-adds-exploited-cisco-catalyst-sd.html)

### 1.5 543,699 still-valid credentials sitting in public GitHub repositories, and Push Protection does not cover half of them (NEW)

**Truffle Security** scanned **224 million repositories and 58+ billion files** from an LLM-training crawl completed **7 August 2025** and found **543,699 unique *still-valid* credentials** across **1.1+ million files and repositories**. The numbers that matter for your own exposure estimate: **median time publicly exposed is 784 days**; roughly **10% of working credentials are older than 6.3 years**, the oldest dating to **2009**; **126,963 Google Cloud service-account credentials** were found of which **69,041 still work**; and of **101,886 npm tokens**, only **one** remained valid — a useful contrast showing that vendor-side automatic revocation is the control that actually works, and that Google Cloud's does not exist in the same form. The GitHub Push Protection finding is the one to brief upward: **199,843 of the exposures (36.8%) happened *after* Push Protection was enabled by default in February 2024**, and **51.8% of live credentials fall into categories Push Protection does not block at all**. Within the categories it does cover, exposure rates fell **53%** — so the control works where it applies and gives false comfort everywhere else. The mechanism also only scans incoming code; **it cannot revoke anything already exposed**. **Action:** do not treat this as someone else's problem based on the dataset being a 2025 crawl — these are *currently valid* credentials, and the long tail means yours are likely among them. Scan **full commit history, not working trees**, across every repository your organisation or its developers own, including personal accounts (see also the PixelLeak item in yesterday's briefing, which is the same exposure surface). Prioritise **cloud service-account keys and database connection strings** over API tokens, since those are the categories with both the worst revocation story and the highest blast radius. Then fix it structurally: **short-lived credentials and OIDC federation instead of long-lived keys**, **automatic secret expiry**, and secret scanning that covers the categories Push Protection does not. Rotating without cleaning history leaves the next credential exposed the same way. **Sources:** [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/over-543-000-valid-credentials-exposed-in-public-github-repositories/) · [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/500000-active-credentials-left-exposed-on-github/) · [GBHackers](https://gbhackers.com/active-credentials-leaked-in-public-github-repos/)
